# Sound effect sources: where recorded sounds come from, and on what terms

Sound effects in an mgpro film are **found, not made**: for each film the agent searches free libraries for human recorded sounds (a real mouse click, a real air swish, real paper), picks a few takes per sound role, and places them like an editor would (`sfx-selection.md`). This file is the map: every free source the research found, what it is good for, how to get files out of it, and the exact clause that decides whether a client ad may use it. It is not a download list. The skill never fetches "all the famous sounds" in advance; it fetches the few files one film needs, and records where each came from.

Everything below was read from the licence pages on **31 October 2026** (`research/free-sfx/report.md` and `research/free-sfx/notes/major_sites_and_licences.md`, `creator_and_niche_packs.md`, `ui_and_cursor_sounds.md`, `motion_sounds.md`). Licences change: four of them changed during 2026 alone (Zapsplat in May, Freesound's AI panel in July, Sonniss v2.0 in August, Pixabay on 20 October). Before a film ships, open the licence page of every source the film used and check the clause below is still there.

A client ad needs three things from a licence at once: commercial use, no attribution (a 15 to 30 s ad has no credit roll), and no rights management registration that could claim the client's upload. Only the first table has all three.

## 4. The clause traps

Five clauses matter more than the word "free".

1. **No stock sound as a sonic logo.** Pixabay bars use "as part of a trade-mark ... or service mark", Adobe bars audio in "logos or ... branding material", CapCut bars use "as a ... logo", and the Content ID bans at Mixkit and Soundsnap point the same way. The sonic logo is always composed for the film (`composition.md`, `logo-resolve.md`); a found sound may sit under it as a non tonal layer, never be it.
2. **No raw files in templates or shared repositories unless CC0.** Mixkit forbids redistributing "in a tool or template", Sonniss forbids a "project template", 99Sounds forbids redistribution "(even for free)", Adobe forbids templates. So a non CC0 file lives only in the film's own `public/sfx/` folder, kept out of git (a `.gitignore` line, with `ASSETS.txt` committed as the manifest), never in `templates/examples/` or the skill repo, and a client receives the rendered film, not the audio folder. Only CC0 files may sit in a repository other people can read.
3. **Pixabay forbids systematic copying.** "Bulk, large-scale or systematic copying ... is strictly prohibited", and automated collection for machine learning too. Pick Pixabay files by hand, a few per film. Mixkit, 99Sounds, BOOM and Zapsplat are browser flows as well: chosen by a person and stored, never scraped.
4. **Fairlight only inside Resolve, CapCut only inside CapCut.** The Fairlight library is licensed only for projects "created and finished using DaVinci Resolve"; CapCut materials may be edited "only on the Platform", and one non commercial item turns the whole project non commercial. Neither can go into a Remotion film.
5. **CC0 and user upload sites promise nothing about provenance.** A Pixabay ambience reportedly failed Epic's content review ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-can-i-prove-its-royalty-free-sound/1251253)); only Sonniss warrants non infringement. Prefer curated sources (Sonniss, Kenney, Mixkit, 99Sounds) and named recordists with a track record (section 5) over anonymous uploads. Content ID risk for short effects is low: the only registrations found concern music tracks (Pixabay's shield icon), and Uppbeat says YouTube's system targets music; the real exposure is a re uploaded commercial sound.

Two smaller ones: Sonniss binds each file to the licence version current on its download date, so the date goes in the record; and on Freesound and SoundBible the licence is per sound, even within one recordist (EminYILDIRIM has CC0, CC BY and CC BY-NC files), so read every page.
