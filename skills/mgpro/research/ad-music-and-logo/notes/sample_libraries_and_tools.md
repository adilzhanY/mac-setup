# Free sample libraries, SFX libraries and headless Linux rendering tools for client commercials

Research date: 2026-10-29. Machine context checked locally: /games has 17 GB free, / has 5.4 GB free (`df -h`). `fluidsynth` is already installed; `pedalboard` and `dawdreamer` are not installed in the system Python.

Licence ranking used below, safest first for a paid client commercial:
1. CC0 / public domain (no credit, no conditions).
2. Permissive custom licence that explicitly allows commercial music and does not exclude advertising (Iowa MIS, Philharmonia, GeneralUser GS, Sonniss GDC, Voxengo IRs, Pixabay).
3. CC BY (legal, but needs a credit somewhere, awkward in a 15 to 30 second ad).
4. CC BY-SA / GPL on samples (legal for music output in practice, but share-alike wording makes lawyers nervous).
5. Excluded for ads: CC Sampling Plus 1.0 (Sonatina, and therefore Virtual Playing Orchestra), EchoThief IRs, Spitfire LABS (no Linux, now Splice).

## Instruments: which free sample libraries are commercially safe, what they contain, how big they are

### Takeaway
The CC0 core is Versilian (VSCO 2 CE and VCSL) plus the Karoryfer and Versilian/Karoryfer SFZ libraries (Osiris Piano, Virtuosity Drums, guitars, basses, all CC0), with FreePats Upright Piano KW (CC0) as a tiny piano. Salamander Grand Piano is CC BY 3.0 (credit needed) and Sonatina Symphonic Orchestra is CC Sampling Plus 1.0, whose licence explicitly excludes advertising, which also taints Virtual Playing Orchestra for commercial ads.

- Repo size about 3.97 GB including history; last push 2026-01-14, so still maintained: [GitHub API, sgossner/VCSL](https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL)

**16. Sonatina Symphonic Orchestra, CC Sampling Plus 1.0, excluded for advertising**
- The licence is retired (CC retired it on 2011-09-12). Verbatim copies may not be shared "in any manner that is primarily intended for or directed toward commercial advantage or private monetary compensation.": [CC Sampling Plus 1.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/sampling+/1.0/legalcode)

**17. Spitfire LABS, not usable here**
- LABS has moved: "Splice INSTRUMENT is the new home for LABS"; a Splice account is required; the page mentions a freemium tier plus subscription and names no plugin formats or operating systems: [Splice INSTRUMENT](https://splice.com/instrument/labs-instrument)
- Spitfire support: recordings made with a paid or free licence of a library such as LABS "will be royalty free": [Spitfire support article](https://support.spitfireaudio.com/en/articles/11815239-are-spitfire-audio-sample-libraries-royalty-free-and-can-i-use-them-on-commercial-recordings)

### Inferences
- For client ads, the cleanest instrument set is CC0 only: VSCO 2 CE and VCSL for strings, pizzicato, mallets, woodwinds and brass; Osiris Piano or Upright Piano KW for piano; Karoryfer guitars and basses; Virtuosity Drums or Big Rusty Drums for a real kit. No credit lines, no share-alike questions.
- CC BY (Salamander piano, MuldjordKit, DRSKit, Naked Drums, Headroom Piano) is legal in ads but needs a credit "reasonable to the medium" (an end card, video description, or invoice note). Treat as second tier.
- Virtual Playing Orchestra is the best sounding free SFZ orchestra on paper, but because it embeds Sonatina samples under Sampling Plus 1.0, which excludes advertising uses, it should not be used for client commercials unless only its VSCO, Iowa and CC0 derived patches are used and verified file by file.
- GeneralUser GS and FluidR3 are fine legally and tiny (32 MB and 145 MB) but GM soundfonts sound dated; use them only for drafts or background layers.
- GPL-licensed drum kits (most Hydrogen kits) are commonly used for music, but only Colombo states a "composition exception"; prefer CC0 kits to avoid the question.
- Disk plan within 17 GB: VSCO 2 CE SFZ (about 2 to 3 GB), Osiris Piano (about 0.5 GB), Upright Piano KW (32 MB), Virtuosity Drums (about 1.2 GB) or Big Rusty Drums (about 0.6 GB), one or two Karoryfer guitars and basses (about 0.5 to 1 GB), GeneralUser GS (32 MB). Total about 5 to 6 GB. VCSL full git clone (about 4 GB with history) is better taken as a shallow clone or individual folders.

### Gaps
- No verified CC0 "felt piano". Pianobook has felt pianos but licences are per uploader and many need full Kontakt; I did not find a CC0 felt piano with a primary licence file.
- Exact unpacked sizes of the VSCO 2 CE 1.1.0 zip and the VCSL 1.2.2 SFZ release were not shown on the release pages (no GitHub assets, Dropbox only).
- I did not get a primary source for Salamander Drumkit licence (only a mirror) or for each FreePats per-instrument licence (mallets, guitars).
- Spitfire LABS EULA text was not retrievable (the terms URL now redirects to Splice). Treat LABS as unavailable on Linux anyway.
- Quality of VCSL celesta, vibraphone and marimba versus Iowa was not assessed from listening tests; no reliable forum comparison found in this pass.

## SFX: high quality free libraries usable without attribution in commercials

### Takeaway
Sonniss GDC bundles are the best real-recording source (royalty free, no attribution, ads explicitly covered) but are huge, so download single parts. Kenney audio packs are CC0 but game-ish; Freesound with a CC0 filter via API is the long tail; Pixabay is no-attribution but under a custom licence; EchoThief and BBC are out.

### Cited Findings
- Sonniss GDC bundles by year: 2024 23.5 GB (9 parts), 2021 to 2023 38.7 GB (14 parts), 2020 48.2 GB (14), 2019 26.2 GB (8), 2018 29.3 GB (8), 2017 19.2 GB (9), 2016 12.9 GB (6), 2015 9.0 GB (5). No GDC 2025 bundle; GDC 2026 has its own page at gdc.sonniss.com: [Sonniss GDC page](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc)
- Download pattern: `https://downloads.sonniss.com/Sonniss.com-GDC2024-GameAudioBundle1of9.zip`; mirrors on feralhosting and your.org; torrents `https://sonniss.com/GameAudioGDCPart[N].torrent`: [Sonniss GDC page](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc)
- Sonniss FAQ summary: "All of the sounds are royalty free and commercially usable." "No attribution is required"; covered projects include "advertisements, trailers"; "Use for AI/ML training is strictly prohibited": [Sonniss GDC page](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc)
- Sonniss licence: "a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use all or any of the sound effects"; "for personal and commercial projects without attribution to the original creator"; "Licensee may not sell any of the sound effects as they come"; may not "distribute, publish, sub-license or otherwise supply the sound effects as sound effects"; prohibited "for the purpose of training artificial intelligence technologies": [Sonniss GDC bundle licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)
- Kenney audio packs: Casino Audio, Sci-fi Sounds, Interface Sounds, Impact Sounds, Voiceover packs, Music Jingles, RPG Audio, Digital Audio, UI Audio: [Kenney audio category](https://kenney.nl/assets/category:Audio). Interface Sounds: licence "Creative Commons CC0", 100 files: [Kenney Interface Sounds](https://kenney.nl/assets/interface-sounds)
- Freesound API: filter syntax `filter=license:"Creative Commons 0"` (license values "Attribution", "Attribution NonCommercial", "Creative Commons 0"); original file download `GET /apiv2/sounds/<id>/download/` "requires OAuth2 authentication"; previews (mp3, ogg) are returned in the `previews` field; throttling per user with burst and sustained limits, `GET /apiv2/usage/` shows them: [Freesound API docs](https://freesound.org/docs/api/resources_apiv2.html)
- Pixabay licence summary: "Use Content for free", "without having to attribute the author", "Modify or adapt"; prohibited: "You cannot sell or distribute Content... on a Standalone basis"; trademarked or branded content cannot be used "for commercial purposes in relation to goods and services"; "It is your responsibility to check whether you require the consent of a third party": [Pixabay licence summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/)

### Inferences
- With 17 GB free, take one Sonniss part at a time (each part is roughly 2.5 to 3.5 GB, from the totals divided by part count), keep only whoosh, impact, foley, paper, wood, mechanical and UI files, then delete the rest. Note the AI training ban: using the files as audio in a rendered ad is fine; feeding them to an ML model is not.
- Freesound CC0 via API is good for targeted gaps (paper rustle, wood knock, typewriter), but downloading originals needs an OAuth2 flow once; previews are lossy MP3/OGG and not suitable as final assets.
- Kenney packs are CC0 and small, ideal for UI clicks and pops, but the character is game UI, less "premium product ad".
- Pixabay's licence is no-attribution but custom, has a branded-content carve-out, and puts third-party rights checking on the user, so it ranks below Sonniss and CC0 Freesound for client work.

### Gaps
- Per-pack sizes and formats for Kenney audio packs were not listed on the pages fetched.
- I did not research ZapSplat (paid tier required for no-attribution, per the brief) or individual sound designer freebie packs; no primary licence texts gathered.
- Sonniss GDC 2026 page contents and size were not fetched.
- Pixabay's full Content License (beyond the summary) and any Content ID behaviour for sound effects were not checked.

## Rendering tools on Linux without a GUI

### Takeaway
`sfizz_render` (SFZ + MIDI file to WAV, BSD-2) plus FluidSynth (SF2, already installed) cover all sample playback from the command line; Pedalboard and DawDreamer load VST3 instruments (sfizz VST3, Surge XT VST3) headless from Python; Surge XT also has its own Python binding (surgepy) built from source.

### Cited Findings
- sfizz: "SFZ parser and synth c++ library", BSD-2-Clause: [sfztools/sfizz repo metadata](https://github.com/sfztools/sfizz). Arch `extra/sfizz` 1.2.3, BSD-2-Clause (`pacman -Si`, local).
- `sfizz_render` usage: `sfizz_render --sfz FILE --wav FILE --midi FILE [OPTIONS]`; options `-s/--samplerate`, `-q/--quality` ("same meaning as the SFZ sample_quality opcode. A value of 1 will use a linear interpolation... higher values will use increasingly better algorithms"), `-p/--polyphony`, `-b/--blocksize`, `--use-eot` ("End the rendering at the last End of Track MIDI message"): [sfizz_render man page source](https://github.com/sfztools/sfizz/blob/develop/clients/sfizz_render.man.in)
- FluidSynth: Arch `extra/fluidsynth` 2.6.1, LGPL-2.1-or-later, already installed at /usr/bin/fluidsynth (local check). Standard headless render: `fluidsynth -ni -F out.wav -r 48000 font.sf2 song.mid`.
- Pedalboard (Spotify), GPL-3.0: "Supports VST3® instrument and effect plugins on macOS, Windows, and Linux"; Linux wheels for x86_64 and aarch64, "x86_64 wheels require AVX support", "Most Linux VSTs require a relatively modern Linux installation (with glibc > 2.27)". Example: `instrument = load_plugin("./VSTs/Magical8BitPlug2.vst3")`; `audio = instrument([Message("note_on", note=60), Message("note_off", note=60, time=5)], duration=5, sample_rate=sample_rate)`: [Pedalboard README](https://github.com/spotify/pedalboard)
- DawDreamer, GPL-3.0: Linux supported (x86_64, aarch64, Python 3.11 to 3.14), VST instruments and effects with state save/load, Faust effects and polyphonic instruments; API `make_plugin_processor`, `add_midi_note(note, velocity, start_sec, duration_sec)`, `load_graph`, `render`, `get_audio`; caveat: import dawdreamer before JAX, Numba or TorchScript or it may segfault: [DawDreamer README](https://github.com/DBraun/DawDreamer)
- Surge XT: GPL-3.0, actively developed (push 2026-10-29): [surge-synthesizer/surge](https://github.com/surge-synthesizer/surge). Arch `extra/surge-xt` 1.3.4, GPL-3.0-only (`pacman -Si`, local). Repo `src/surge-python` contains `setup.py`, `pyproject.toml`, `surgepy.cpp`: [surge-python folder](https://github.com/surge-synthesizer/surge/tree/main/src/surge-python)
- surgepy is installed by cloning the Surge repo and pip installing `surge-python` from source; it renders to an array then a WAV; "at most one SurgeSynthesizer is set up in a given Python process" (per the jird wrapper docs): [jird docs, Surge](https://jird.readthedocs.io/en/latest/surge.html)
- Carla 2.5.10 (GPL-2.0-or-later, 80 MiB) and LMMS 1.2.2 (GPL-2.0-or-later) are in Arch extra (`pacman -Si`, local).
- Voxengo free reverb IRs: "Permission is granted to anyone to use these impulse files royalty-free for any purpose, including commercial usage."; "You may not sell these impulse files or earn any direct or indirect profit from their distribution." 38 IRs, 44.1 kHz 16 bit WAV, 6.9 MB, `https://www.voxengo.com/files/impulses/IMreverbs.zip`: [Voxengo impulses](https://www.voxengo.com/impulses/)
- EchoThief licence: "You are welcome to use the EchoThief Impulse Response Library to create derivative work (such as convolving it with other sounds to create reverberation). If you would like to use it in any other way, let's talk.": [EchoThief License PDF](https://www.cs.bu.edu/fac/snyder/cs583/Homeworks/ImpulseResponses/EchoThiefImpulseResponseLibrary/EchoThief%20License.pdf)
- OpenAIR: a third-party dataset card lists OpenAIR 1.0.0 as CC-BY-4.0: [audb OpenAIR card](https://audeering.github.io/audbcards/audb-public/openair.html); the OpenAIR site itself could not be fetched (expired TLS certificate during this research).

### Inferences
- Simplest robust pipeline: Python writes a MIDI file per stem (mido or pretty_midi), shells out to `sfizz_render --sfz X.sfz --midi stem.mid --wav stem.wav -s 48000 -q 3 --use-eot` for SFZ instruments and `fluidsynth -ni -F` for SF2, then mixes and adds reverb with Pedalboard (`Convolution` with a Voxengo IR, plus compressor and limiter) in Python. No plugin hosting needed for samples.
- For synth pads, load Surge XT's VST3 (from the Arch package) with Pedalboard `load_plugin` and render MIDI messages; surgepy is an alternative but needs a source build. GPL on the synth does not apply to the audio it outputs (general GPL understanding, not stated in the sources fetched).
- EchoThief allows convolution ("derivative work") but asks for contact for other uses; its wording is less clear than Voxengo's, so Voxengo plus OpenAIR (CC BY, needs credit) or self-generated algorithmic reverb is safer.
- Carla and LMMS add little over sfizz_render plus Pedalboard for a headless Python pipeline.

### Gaps
- Whether the Arch `sfizz` package (installed size 1.33 KiB, so a split meta package) ships `sfizz_render` and the VST3 build was not verified; may require `sfizz-lv2`/`sfizz-vst3` split packages or a source build.
- Whether Surge XT 1.3.4 ships a CLI renderer (surge-xt-cli) was not confirmed; no primary documentation found.
- Vital: licence for its presets and wavetables, and headless use via Pedalboard, were not researched in this pass.
- Pedalboard does not list LV2 support (VST3 and AU only); DawDreamer also does not mention LV2.
- OpenAIR per-IR licences could not be read from the primary site.

## Best combination for realistic results per instrument

### Takeaway
Use sfizz_render with CC0 SFZ libraries as the backbone, add CC BY Salamander only when a concert grand is essential and a credit is acceptable, and use Surge XT via Pedalboard for pads, with Voxengo IR convolution for space.

### Cited Findings
- Grand piano: Salamander V3, 16 velocity layers, 48 kHz / 24 bit, CC BY 3.0: [SFZ Instruments, Salamander](https://sfzinstruments.github.io/pianos/salamander). CC0 alternative: Osiris Piano (Versilian and Karoryfer): [sfzinstruments GitHub organisation](https://github.com/sfzinstruments). Tiny CC0 upright: Upright Piano KW, 2 velocity layers, 32 MiB FLAC: [FreePats](https://freepats.zenvoid.org/Piano/acoustic-grand-piano.html)
- Strings ostinato and pizzicato: VSCO 2 CE (CC0 orchestral library): [VSCO-2-CE](https://github.com/sgossner/VSCO-2-CE); low pizz: Karoryfer Sneakybass, pizzicato double bass, CC0: [sfzinstruments GitHub organisation](https://github.com/sfzinstruments); cello: Karoryfer x Bigcat Cello, CC0: [sfzinstruments GitHub organisation](https://github.com/sfzinstruments)
- Marimba, vibraphone, xylophone, bells, crotales: Iowa MIS ("without restrictions"): [Iowa MIS](https://theremin.music.uiowa.edu/mis.html); VCSL (CC0, wholetone sampled, percussion with more velocity layers and round robins "where warranted (e.g. percussion)"): [VCSL README](https://github.com/sgossner/VCSL)
- Guitar: Black And Green Guitars, Emilyguitar, Shinyguitar, all CC0: [sfzinstruments GitHub organisation](https://github.com/sfzinstruments)
- Bass: Black And Blue Basses, Fashionbass, Growlybass, Ergo, all CC0: [sfzinstruments GitHub organisation](https://github.com/sfzinstruments)
- Real drum kit: Virtuosity Drums (CC0, about 1.2 GB) or Big Rusty Drums (CC0, about 0.6 GB); more realism with CC BY 4.0 Naked Drums ("10 round-robins, up to 5 velocity layers and several mic layers"): [sfzinstruments GitHub organisation](https://github.com/sfzinstruments)
- Pads: Surge XT VST3 via Pedalboard `load_plugin`: [Pedalboard README](https://github.com/spotify/pedalboard); [surge-synthesizer/surge](https://github.com/surge-synthesizer/surge)

### Inferences
- Realism ranking for an ad on this machine: piano (Salamander or Osiris) and drums (Virtuosity or Naked Drums) will be the most convincing; VSCO 2 CE strings are good for short ostinato and pizz but thin for long legato; mallets from Iowa/VCSL are convincing because mallet instruments sample well; guitar strums will be the weakest (single note samples), so favour picked or muted guitar lines.
- Realism comes as much from rendering choices as from samples: humanised MIDI velocity and timing, round robin aware SFZ, `-q 3` or higher resampling in sfizz_render, a shared convolution room on all stems, and a bus compressor and limiter in Pedalboard.

### Gaps
- No listening comparison or forum benchmark was found in this pass to rank Osiris versus Salamander, or Virtuosity versus Naked Drums; rankings above are inferred from sampling depth (layers, round robins, mics) shown in the sources.
- No CC0 felt piano, celesta or sampled analog synth pad library was confirmed from a primary source.
