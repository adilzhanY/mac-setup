# Composition craft for 15 to 60 s product commercials and motion graphics films

Scope note for the report writer: primary sources that publish note-level detail about named ads are rare. Studios describe process and mood, almost never chords or BPM. Every bullet under "Cited Findings" has a source. Bullets under "Inferences" are either arithmetic derived from cited facts or standard composition and orchestration practice that I could not tie to a single citable page in this session; they are marked "(craft practice, unsourced)" where that applies, so the writer can decide how to present them. Several key sites (Packt chapter pages, LBB Online, Boing Boing, Soundfly) returned HTTP 403, so some findings rely on search snippets of those pages and are flagged.

## 1. Structure of a 15/30/45/60 s cue and editing music to picture

### Takeaway
Working ad composers lock the timing skeleton (tempo, tempo shifts, hit points, meter changes) against the locked cut before writing melody, then compose "to and away from" a small number of moments and end exactly on the frame, ringout included. A constant tempo can still put every hit on a downbeat if the tempo is chosen by arithmetic.

### Cited Findings
- Composer Jorde Heys (AFTRS) on a 30 s promo: "30 seconds isn't a huge amount of time," so getting the musical pacing to "work with, interplay, or move in counterpoint with the edit and cuts is crucial." He spends time "locking down the tempo, any tempo shifts, hit points, and time signature changes" while melodic ideas are still "bubbling away." [AFTRS](https://www.aftrs.edu.au/blog/mastering-the-score-jorde-heys-on-crafting-music-to-brief/)
- Heys' arrangement map for the AFTRS Open Day spot: front half mainly synth based, a hard cut before the spot's only line of dialogue, then a back half that moves into an orchestral world, the soft opening setting up "a punchier orchestral push towards the end" to land the main message. He places musical moments to compose "to and away from," and builds development and climax "through texture and dynamics." Strict time limits "force me to make decisive choices." [AFTRS](https://www.aftrs.edu.au/blog/mastering-the-score-jorde-heys-on-crafting-music-to-brief/)
- Keith Kenniff (Helios, Goldmund; ads for Apple iPhone, Google, Facebook, Toyota, Prudential, Quaker Oats) calls his ad work "mini film scores" rather than jingles: "A lot of what I do is trying to tell stories in a very, very short amount of time." A Toyota brief asked for a piece that was spooky and ambient in the first half and upbeat and poppy in the second, and the job was making the two halves one piece. [NPR / KUER](https://www.kuer.org/2012-08-26/composer-keith-kenniff-keeps-the-ads-humming)
- Textbook practice (Chris Piorkowski, "Scoring to Picture in Logic Pro", Packt, chapter 10 "Scoring a Commercial"): an example commercial cue uses a constant tempo of 99 BPM and still places its hit points on the downbeat of a bar without beat mapping; chapter 12 covers outlining structure and timings, and chapter 7 tempo maps. (From search snippets; the pages themselves returned 403.) [Packt ch.10](https://packtpub.com/en-mx/product/scoring-to-picture-in-logic-pro-9781837636891/chapter/chapter-10-scoring-a-commercial-14/section/chapter-10-scoring-a-commercial-14); [Packt ch.12](https://packtpub.com/en-lt/product/scoring-to-picture-in-logic-pro-9781837636891/chapter/chapter-12-advanced-concepts-for-dealing-with-timing-in-film-music-16/section/outlining-structure-and-timings-ch16lvl1sec00)
- Hit point technique: note the exact timecode of the event, choose a tempo (often refined with a tempo map) so a strong beat falls on that frame, then derive the click from it. [IRPR Sound glossary](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/glossary/hit-point/)
- Older commercial practice: composers used printed reference books listing BPM against durations to find tempos that land hit points (anecdotal column). [Mix](https://www.mixonline.com/blog/mix-blog-studio-residual-knowledge)
- Spot timing discipline: use meter and tempo to fit the voice track, highlight key points, and end precisely at 30 s including the ringout (university radio spot assignment, not a composer interview). [PDX](https://web.pdx.edu/~jnewton/assignments/69rs_radio_spot/index.html)
- A 30 to 60 s window forces the composer to be "economical" with musical choices. [NextTV / MBPT Spotlight](https://www.nexttv.com/post-type/mbpt-spotlight-note-note-creating-perfect-underscore-music-adds-value-commercials-129640)
- Hans Zimmer's theatrical Netflix ident is a worked example of a long "build to logo": about 16 s, built from tremolo strings and rumbling percussion, landing on the standard ta-dum at the end, commissioned because the 3 s original felt too rushed in a cinema. [Classic FM](https://www.classicfm.com/composers/zimmer/netflix-extended-cinema-ta-dum-sound/)

### Inferences
- Tempo arithmetic that follows from the "hit on the downbeat" method. A hit at time t lands on beat n when BPM = 60 x n / t. In 4/4: at 120 BPM a bar is 2.0 s (30 s = 15 bars); at 96 BPM a bar is 2.5 s (30 s = 12 bars, 15 s = 6 bars, 60 s = 24 bars); at 100 BPM a bar is 2.4 s (30 s = 12.5 bars); at 90 BPM a bar is 2.667 s; at 80 BPM a bar is 3.0 s (30 s = 10 bars). 96 and 120 BPM are the "clean" choices because 15/30/60 s become whole bars. For a code composer at 30 fps, one beat at 96 BPM is 18.75 frames, at 120 BPM exactly 15 frames, at 100 BPM 18 frames, at 90 BPM 20 frames, at 128 BPM 14.06 frames. Tempos whose beat is a whole number of frames give frame exact beats: at 30 fps that is 90, 100, 120 and 150 BPM; at 60 fps 80 BPM (45 frames) also works; 96 BPM is never whole (18.75 or 37.5 frames), so round each hit to the nearest frame.
- Practical cue map for a 30 s spot at 96 BPM in 4/4 (12 bars) (craft practice, unsourced): bars 1 to 2 (0 to 5 s) establish one texture and the motif fragment; bars 3 to 6 (5 to 15 s) add a second layer and the full motif; bar 7 (15 to 17.5 s) is a turn or "breath" (drop layers, half time, or a 2/4 bar) that sits on the main cut or reveal; bars 8 to 10 (17.5 to 25 s) are the fullest section with counter line; bars 11 to 12 (25 to 30 s) clear space for the logo: one hit or chord on the logo frame, then 1.5 to 2.5 s of ring. Supers and voice lines get the thinnest bars.
- 15 s: usually 6 to 8 bars, one idea, no development; motif, one lift, logo. 45 to 60 s: room for one real development (key lift, reharmonization, or a half time switch) and an A to A' return.
- Meter changes are the usual way to "catch" a cut that does not fall on a barline: a 2/4 or 3/4 bar inserted before the cut, or a 5/4 or 7/8 bar, keeps the downbeat on the picture. Rubato and tempo ramps (for example 92 to 100 BPM over 4 bars) are used when the edit accelerates. (craft practice, unsourced)
- The button/stinger: the last musical event lands on the logo frame (or 1 to 2 frames before it, so the transient is perceived on the frame), then the tail rings out inside the spot length. Leave at least 1 to 2 s for tail plus any legal end card. (craft practice, unsourced)

## 2. Melody and motif: length, intervals, contour, development

### Takeaway
Brand motifs are short (2 to 5 notes, roughly 1 to 4 s), often set to the brand name's syllables, and built from a few wide, stable intervals (fourths, fifths, octaves) or a single strong rhythm. Research shows complexity helps recognition but hurts sung recall, so the motif should be simple in pitch and distinctive in timbre or rhythm. Development across a spot is done by changing instrumentation, tempo and genre while keeping pitch and rhythm identity.

### Cited Findings

Pitches come from a forum post and a German tech article that agree; Intel itself confirms only "five notes" and that Werzowa made "20 sounds from those notes." - Werzowa has re-arranged the bong repeatedly to keep it current, including a 2015 mashup with Beethoven's 5th. [Wikipedia: Walter Werzowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Werzowa)
- Netflix ta-dum (Lon Bender with Netflix VP Todd Yellin): the main knock is Bender's wedding ring on a cabinet, layered with a slowed anvil and muted hits, plus a reversed electric guitar swell for the tail. Bender made roughly 20 to 30 candidates; a goat bleat was a finalist; focus groups linked the chosen one to "dramatic" and "movie." [Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.com/netflix-ta-dum-sound-almost-120045836.html); [Twenty Thousand Hertz](https://www.20k.org/episodes/netflix); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/the-netflix-ta-dum-sound-features-a-reversed-electric-guitar-sample-that-was-created-using-90s-effect-pedals)
- YouTube "Buckle Up" sonic logo (Antfood, with BUCK animation): about 3 s, in order: a "rich, diving bass," a "vibrant upward glissando," a two-note melodic signature matching the two syllables of "YouTube" with an organic timbre and a champagne-pop accent on "You," a door-unlocking tactile texture for momentum, and a warm synth pad playing a major 7th chord under the melody. [Mastercard newsroom](https://newsroom.mastercard.com/news/press/2019/february/sound-on-mastercard-debuts-sonic-brand/); [Muse by Clio](https://musebyclios.com/?p=48490); [Linkinpedia](https://www.linkinpedia.com/wiki/Master_Soundscape)

### Inferences
- Intel interval analysis (derived from the cited pitches): Db Db Gb Db Ab is unison, up a perfect 4th, down a perfect 4th, up a perfect 5th. Only two interval types, all perfect consonances, implying I to IV and ending on the 5th (open, not closed). Rhythm carries most of the identity. That fits the Cambridge result: simple pitch content for recall, distinct timbre and rhythm for recognition.
- YouTube pattern as a template: low anchor, rising gesture, 2-note melody on the name syllables, maj7 pad. A maj7 (or add9) under the final note sounds "open and modern" because the 7th sits a half step below the octave and never fully closes. (craft practice, unsourced)
- Motif rules for a code composer (craft practice, unsourced):
  - 2 to 5 notes, under about 3 s at logo tempo, one rhythmic hook (a dotted figure, an anticipation, or a short-long).
  - Lead with a leap (4th, 5th, 6th or octave) then step back inside it; a leap followed by stepwise return in the opposite direction is the most "singable" shape. Avoid pure stepwise scale runs (sound like a doorbell or a stock riser) and avoid ending on the tonic of a root position triad if the ad wants "openness".
  - End on scale degree 5, 2 or 3 over the tonic chord for "open/forward", on 1 for "closed/trusted".
  - Fit the brand name's syllable count and stress (Intel, YouTube, CBS all do this).
- Developing one motif across 30 s (craft practice, unsourced): statement in a soft timbre (bars 1 to 4); same pitches in augmentation or with a displaced rhythm (start on the "and" of 1) under a new chord (reharmonize the same melody note: e.g. melody note E over C, then over Am, then over Fmaj7 where it becomes the 7th, then over A/C#); fragmentation (first 2 notes only, as a riff or counter line); inversion only as a counter melody, never as the final statement; final statement in the original form at the logo, usually in a brighter register or new timbre. Mastercard's "same 8 bars, new instruments and tempo" is the brand-system version of this.

## 3. Harmony: progressions, voicings, modal colour, clichés

### Takeaway
Sourced analysis of ad harmony is thin. What is sourced: the corporate stock sound is a known cliché (ukulele, glockenspiel, claps, "basic rules only"); I-V-vi-IV is widely described as the most used pop progression; Thomas Newman style (the model for much "thoughtful tech" scoring) uses ostinati, very few chords (I and IV for 16 bars), Dorian colour, maj7 and min9 dissonances, half step tension and major/minor juxtaposition; Lydian's raised 4th is the film shorthand for wonder. Fresh ad harmony comes from voicing and colour on simple progressions, not from complex progressions.

### Cited Findings
- I-V-vi-IV is described as "said to be the most used progression in modern music" (secondary, no percentage). [Native Instruments](https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/common-chord-progressions); [Berklee Online](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/common-chord-progressions-and-how-to-make-them-your-own/). Hooktheory also keeps a "corniest progression of all time" trends page. [Hooktheory](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab/view/progression-test/corniest-progression-of-all-time)
- Hooktheory data on minor keys: chord shares i 14%, VI 9%, VII 9%, III 6%, iv 5%, v 3%; most minor progressions start on i. [Hooktheory blog](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/minor-chord-progressions/)
- Corporate cliché: Boing Boing's "How to make bland, soulless corporate music" calls the ukulele "the most common and egregious isn't-life-just-awesome instrument"; a commenter: corporate music follows "the basic composition rules, and only the basic composition rules." (Via search snippet; page returned 403.) [Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2019/10/23/how-to-make-bland-soulless-co.html). Stock libraries sell the ukulele + glockenspiel + hand-claps bundle explicitly "for corporate presentations, advertising, tutorials." [MonsterONE](https://monsterone.com/stock-music/independent-ukulele-cheerful-acoustic-commercial-pop-stock-music-o287302/)
- Thomas Newman: called "eclectic minimalism," because repeated short figures (ostinato) are central; in Saving Mr. Banks the first sixteen bars use only I and IV, with inner voices close to tonic and dominant pitches. [Film Music Notes](https://filmmusicnotes.com/oscar-nominees-2014-best-original-score-part-2-of-6-thomas-newmans-saving-mr-banks/)
- Rick Beato on Newman: Shawshank "Stoic Theme" uses an opening ostinato in C Dorian with dissonant voicings including major 7ths and minor 9ths; tension comes from half step movement within chords and juxtaposing major and minor at once. [Beato, Stoic Theme](https://rosetta.to/u/rickbeato/thomas-newman-stoic-theme-shawshank-redemption-breakdown); [Beato, Write like Newman](https://rosetta.to/u/rickbeato/how-to-write-like-thomas-newman-secrets-of-film-scoring-part-1)
- A Newman analysis (1917) describes an ostinato juxtaposing major and minor thirds, starting on piano and passed to strings for the climax; a 2006 ANPPOM paper lists parallel fifths (especially in piano) and deceptive cadences among his techniques. [Scribd notes](https://www.scribd.com/document/487936862/Presentation-Notes); [ANPPOM 2006](https://anppom.org.br/anais/anaiscongresso_anppom_2006/CDROM/COM/07_Com_TeoComp/sessao01/07COM_TeoComp_0101-156.pdf)
- Stock music is sold "in the style of Thomas Newman" with piano, strings and marimba, which shows how far that palette has become a template. [Shockwave-Sound](https://shockwave-sound.com/stock-music-track/14392/distant-horizon-lp)
- Lydian: the raised 4th removes the pull of the perfect 4th and gives "weightless suspension"; cited film uses include E.T., Superman, Toy Story 3; the I to II major movement is its signature. Sources are tutorial grade. [Mark Slater](https://markslater.net/unlocking-the-power-of-the-lydian-mode/); [Beato, Lydian](https://rosetta.to/u/rickbeato/the-lydian-mode-why-film-composers-and-rock-guitarists-love-this-sound); [Guitar Composers Toolbox](https://www.guitarcomposerstoolbox.com/scales-for-film-composition/)
- YouTube's sonic logo puts a major 7th pad under its two note melody. [Antfood](https://antfood.com/labs/crafting-youtubes-first-sonic-brand)
- Zimmer's Netflix theatrical ident is described (opinion, mirror site) as moving between major and minor centres for suspense. [mirror](https://atepxxuku.pages.dev/posts/netflix-extended-theme-hans-zimmer-turned-the-ba-dum-into-more)

### Inferences
All below is craft practice, unsourced, offered as a usable harmonic kit consistent with the cited Newman, Lydian and maj7 findings.
- Clichés to avoid as written: I-V-vi-IV and vi-IV-I-V in root position block triads on every downbeat, one chord per bar, at 110 to 128 BPM with claps on 2 and 4; the "uplifting corporate" move of ending every 4 bars on V then crashing on I; a final root position major triad with a cymbal.
- Same function, fresher colour (C major examples):
  - Fmaj9 (F A C E G) to C/E to Am11 to Gsus4 (add9). Same as IV-I-vi-V but with inversions, 9ths and a sus that never resolves.
  - Lydian loop: Cmaj7 to D/C (or D add9/C) over a C pedal. Two chords for 8 bars is enough; "wonder," product reveals.
  - Mixolydian: C to Bb/C to F/C over a C pedal (I, bVII, IV). Confident, warm, "human" (sport, outdoors, Afro and Latin grooves).
  - Dorian minor: Dm9 to G/D (or G13) over D pedal (i to IV major). Cool, focused, tech-noir, Newman Shawshank area.
  - Borrowed iv: C to Fm/C (or Fm6) to C. One borrowed chord near the end gives "bittersweet, earned" (lifestyle and emotional films).
  - Chromatic mediant for the logo: from C go to Ab maj7 or E major for the reveal, then back to C add9 or simply stay. Sounds expensive because it is unexpected but voice-leads by common tone.
  - Newman style stasis: two chords (I and IV, or Imaj7 and IVmaj7#11) for 16 bars, with movement carried by ostinato and inner voices.
- Pedal points: hold the bass (or a high piano octave) on the tonic or dominant while upper chords change. Creates "forward but grounded" and lets harmonies that would clash in root position (e.g. Bb/C, D/C) sound modern.
- Voicing that sounds expensive: wide intervals low (octave or fifth below about C3, no 3rds below about E3), close clusters high (2nds and 9ths between C4 and C6); put the 3rd in the middle register or omit it (sus2, 5ths) for neutrality; keep the top voice moving by step while the bass moves by 4th/5th or stays as a pedal; common tones held between chords; parallel fifths in piano (cited above) for the "open" Newman/indie sound.
- Key changes: the "truck driver" half or whole step up is the most clichéd lift. Fresher options: shift to the relative major or minor, to bVI, or drop out the bass for a bar and re-enter a 4th lower. In 30 s, usually do not modulate at all; use a register lift instead.
- Endings with logo: resolved = I add9 or Imaj7 (warm, trusted); unresolved = IVmaj7, Isus2, or ending on the 5th or 9th in the melody over a tonic pedal (forward looking, "to be continued"). Intel ends on the 5th; YouTube ends on a maj7 colour (both cited above).

## 4. Rhythm and groove

### Takeaway
Very little sourced material addresses ad rhythm directly. Sourced points: rhythm in short spots is driven by the cut's pacing and the chosen tempo (Heys), motifs take their rhythm from the brand name's syllables (Intel, YouTube), and stock "corporate" groove is part of the cliché. A static, quantized loop sounds cheap because it ignores the edit; human feel comes from accent patterns, micro timing and phrasing at 2 and 4 bar levels.

### Cited Findings
- Rhythm in a short ad score comes mainly from the cut's pacing and tempo choice; harmony and melody come mainly from palette. [AFTRS / Jorde Heys](https://www.aftrs.edu.au/blog/mastering-the-score-jorde-heys-on-crafting-music-to-brief/)
- Intel's rhythm follows "In-tel In-side." [t3n](https://t3n.de/news/?p=1627039)
- Newman's identity rests on repeated short figures (ostinato) rather than drum grooves. [Film Music Notes](https://filmmusicnotes.com/oscar-nominees-2014-best-original-score-part-2-of-6-thomas-newmans-saving-mr-banks/)
- Synthetic or MIDI heavy emulation is offered by stock sellers themselves as the reason corporate tracks sound "cheesy" (seller claim, weak). [MonsterONE listing via search](https://monsterone.com/stock-music/everyday-uplifting-and-positive-corporate-easy-listening-stock-music-with-ukulele-o309533)

### Inferences
All craft practice, unsourced.
- Tempo by mood (BPM, quarter note): calm/wellness/premium reveal 60 to 80 (or 120 to 160 felt in half time); thoughtful tech, piano and ostinato 84 to 100; confident product, indie electronic 100 to 118; energetic launch, sport, Afro/Latin 118 to 128; hype cut-downs and social 128 to 150 or half time feels at 140 to 170.
- Why a four-on-the-floor loop sounds cheap: identical velocity, identical sample, no fills, the same 1 bar repeated for 15 bars, and no change at the picture's turning points. Fix: write 2 and 4 bar phrases with a variation in bar 4 (fill, drop of the kick, open hat), and make arrangement changes on cuts.
- Humanising in code: velocity spread of roughly 15 to 25% on hats and shakers with a fixed accent pattern (e.g. strong on 1 and the "and" of 2); ghost snare notes at 20 to 35% velocity on "e" and "a" 16ths; micro timing of a few ms (snare slightly late for laid back, hats slightly early for drive); 16th swing in the 54 to 60% range for lift without obvious shuffle; vary the sample per hit (round robin).
- Syncopation that reads as modern: kick on 1 and the "and" of 2 (or 3 and the "a" of 3), claps or rims on 2 and 4 only in the full section; 3+3+2 eighth note grouping in a 4/4 bar (tresillo) for Afro, Latin and dancehall flavour; 3 against 2 or 3 against 4 in shakers and marimba; dotted eighth delay as a rhythmic layer.
- Half time switch: keep tempo, move the snare from 2 and 4 to 3 only, for a "weight" moment on a hero shot, then return to full time for the payoff. Double time hats are the opposite gesture.
- Odd groupings: one bar of 7/8 or 5/4 to catch a cut, or a 7/8 ostinato over 4/4 drums for restless "innovation" energy.

## 5. Arrangement and dynamics

### Takeaway
Ad composers build energy by changing palette and texture across the spot (synth to orchestra, ambient to pop, soft to "punchier push"), not by raising level alone, and they clear space for dialogue and supers. Logo moments are designed as a small dramatic arc of contrasts (low to high, close to far, dark to bright).

### Cited Findings
- Development and climax "through texture and dynamics"; synth front half, orchestral back half; soft opening, "punchier orchestral push" toward the message; hard cut before the only dialogue line. [AFTRS / Jorde Heys](https://www.aftrs.edu.au/blog/mastering-the-score-jorde-heys-on-crafting-music-to-brief/)
- Toyota brief realised by Kenniff: spooky ambient first half, upbeat pop second half in one piece. Prudential: sparse, quiet, ambient, experimental; he placed leaves on piano strings so each leaf gave a different tone, to match handmade visuals. [NPR / KUER](https://www.kuer.org/2012-08-26/composer-keith-kenniff-keeps-the-ads-humming)
- YouTube logo arc: low dive, upward glissando, two note melody, tactile texture, maj7 pad; contrasts small to large, close to far, dark to bright, intimate to bold; "a clear beginning and end." [Antfood](https://antfood.com/labs/crafting-youtubes-first-sonic-brand)
- Zimmer's Netflix theatrical ident: tremolo strings plus rumbling percussion build for about 16 s into the standard ta-dum. [Classic FM](https://www.classicfm.com/composers/zimmer/netflix-extended-cinema-ta-dum-sound/)
- Newman: ostinato starts on piano and is passed to strings for the climax (handoff as a build device). [Scribd notes](https://www.scribd.com/document/487936862/Presentation-Notes)
- Temp tracks should be treated as a "jumping off point" for something original. [Audient](https://audient.com/de/tutorial/composing-commercials/)

### Inferences
All craft practice, unsourced.
- Instrument roles and registers: sub/bass (below C2 to C3) on roots or pedal only; pulse/ostinato (C4 to C6: piano, marimba, plucked synth, muted guitar) in 8ths or 16ths; pad or strings sustain (C3 to C5) on chord tones; melody/motif an octave above or below the ostinato, never in the same register; air layer (C6 and up: bells, shimmer, harmonics) only in the peak and the logo. Leave 300 Hz to 3 kHz open under voice over.
- Energy without volume: add rhythmic density (8ths to 16ths), widen register (add a low octave and a high octave), change articulation (staccato to legato or vice versa), move pads from close to wide voicing, add a counter melody in contrary motion, switch from synth to acoustic (or the reverse) at the turn.
- Lift versus drop: a lift adds layers and register gradually to a bigger restatement (warm, emotional, product story); a drop removes everything for 1 to 2 beats (often a riser or reversed swell into silence) then slams back with the full groove on a cut (hype, launch, social). In 15 to 30 s use at most one of either.
- Call and response: motif in piano answered by the same rhythm in plucks or vocal chops a bar later; in the final section the answer becomes the logo hit.
- Ending on the logo: pull the groove out 1 to 2 beats before the logo (a "gap"), then one combined hit (low thump plus chord plus motif's last note) on the logo frame, tail 1.5 to 3 s. Resolved chord for trust brands, sus2/maj7/add9 for forward looking tech.

## 6. Contemporary styles in 2020 to 2026 tech and design brand films

### Takeaway
Sourced material on named recent ad scores is sparse. What the sources show: Apple uses a mix of licensed songs, arranged classical pieces and original pieces (Beethoven Piano Trio arranged by Found Objects for Apple Music Classical; a Hannah Peel original in a 2025 Apple ad); the piano plus ambient "mini film score" sound of Keith Kenniff was a long running ad staple for Apple, Google and Facebook; Newman style piano, strings and marimba minimalism is now a stock template; trade commentary urges choosing unexpected genres.

### Inferences
Craft practice, unsourced; useful as style definitions for a code composer.
- Solo/felt piano minimalism: 70 to 90 BPM, felt piano with audible hammer and pedal noise, 2 to 4 chords with added 9ths, right hand broken 8ths, left hand octaves; close mic, little reverb. "Honest, human, thoughtful."
- String ostinati (neo classical): 8th or 16th note spiccato or pulsing cello/viola on a pedal, violins enter with long tones over it, harmony moving by step over the pedal (Glass and Richter descended; Newman's piano to strings handoff). Build by adding octaves.
- Indie electronic: 100 to 120 BPM, plucked synths with short decay and dotted 8th delay, warm analog bass, chopped vocal syllables as melody, live sounding drums with ghost notes, mixolydian or lydian loops.
- Organic percussion and Afro/Latin grooves: shakers, congas, log drums, claps in 3+3+2 or 12/8 against 4/4, 110 to 124 BPM, mixolydian vamps, human voice chants; used to signal "global, human, joyful" without ukulele.
- Glitch / UI music: stuttered and reversed fragments of the motif, bit crushed clicks synced to on screen UI events, sine and FM bells, short silences on cuts; works for product UI demos because sound events double as interface sound design.
- Marimba and mallet minimalism: interlocking 16th patterns between two mallet parts (one on beats, one off beats), Newman style; now risks sounding like stock if paired with ukulele, glockenspiel and claps.
