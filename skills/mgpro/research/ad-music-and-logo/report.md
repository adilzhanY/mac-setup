# Shaped variation, not polish, makes ads feel human

Across music, sound, logo motion and cursor work, the evidence points to one rule: **audiences flag sameness and blends, not imperfection**. Generated and code-made audio gives itself away through identical repetition (the same sample on every note, the same envelope, the same room depth, the same palette), while human work varies for a reason: a bow running out, a phrase reaching its peak, a cadence, a hand slowing into a target. Listeners pick AI songs from human ones at chance on random pairs (53%) and only reach 66% on closely matched pairs, citing vocals, sound quality and things that are "too perfect" ([Echoes of Humanity, arXiv 2509.25601](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25601)). For an instrumental score made in code, realism therefore comes from instrument, performance and space: recorded samples with real velocity layers and round robins, systematic rather than random timing, and a shared convolution room. A short CC0 sample core (VSCO 2 CE, VCSL, the Karoryfer SFZ libraries, Sonniss for effects) rendered headless with `sfizz_render` and Pedalboard replaces most pure synthesis. The last film's other failures share the same root. Its audio dropouts came from contrast made with a gain change (music multiplied to zero, tails and all) instead of with arrangement. Its house to cube morph showed two images blended by opacity instead of one object changing. Its cursor faded in place and moved with one symmetric curve. The fixes: arranged breaths with a bed under them, one decisive object transformation that decelerates into the mark with the sonic accent on the landing frame and a 1.5 to 3 s hold, a visible change every 1 to 3 s with the product in the first 2 s, and a cursor whose duration follows distance, whose ease is asymmetric and whose click has weight.

## The ear flags identical repetition, not imperfection

Each family of artificial audio has its own fingerprint, but they share one cause. Suno and Udio output is described as having a metallic shimmer on cymbals and sibilants, a "watery" swirl on sustained notes and smeared transients, attributed to the neural codec the generator decodes through ([Intrect](https://intrect.io/blog/suno-artifacts/)). Its arrangements fall back on defaults with predictable chord moves ([James Palm](https://james-palm.medium.com/10-suno-ai-mistakes-that-make-your-songs-sound-like-everyone-elses-b4c8a2784e33)), and one test produced an awkward 12 bar first verse where a writer would use 8 or 16 ([Substack](https://wewillnotbeflattened.substack.com/p/i-asked-generative-ai-music-platform-suno-to-write-a-podcast-theme-things-got-weird-a66820103f55)). MIDI mockups give themselves away through the **"machine gun" effect**, the identical recording played on every trigger ([SampleStack](https://samplestack.app/news/round-robin-explained/)), through dynamics drawn as straight fader ramps and one unbroken sustain with no re-bow ([Composer Workflow](https://agents.composerworkflow.com/academy/humanizing-a-mockup/)), and through one reverb on every track, which puts every instrument at one distance ([modwheel.net](https://modwheel.net/guides/reverb-for-cinematic-mixing)). Stock corporate music is competent but "engineered to be inoffensive... designed to fit everything, which means they say nothing" ([Artyfile](https://artyfile.com/blog/why-your-commercial-sounds-like-everyone-elses)), with the ukulele, glockenspiel and claps bundle sold explicitly "for corporate presentations, advertising" ([MonsterONE](https://monsterone.com/stock-music/independent-ukulele-cheerful-acoustic-commercial-pop-stock-music-o287302/)). Oscillator sound fails in its own ways. Synth brass has no initial burst of air, so it needs a transient whose harmonic mix changes, plus velocity-dependent brightness ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/synthesizing-brass-instruments)). A constant triangle LFO on a string ensemble "sounds unnaturally regular" until random modulation is added ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/synthesizing-strings-string-machines)).

The common thread is sameness: same waveform per note, same depth per instrument, same palette per brief, same LFO cycle. The detection research explains why this matters more than spectral fingerprints. Machine detectors catch current AI music above 97 to 99% by finding periodic spectral peaks left by upsampling layers ([Afchar et al., arXiv 2506.19108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19108)). But they degrade on short excerpts and music under speech or effects ([arXiv 2602.06823](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.06823)), and a 2026 classroom test of 3,210 responses found humans at **48.16%, indistinguishable from chance** ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/22278782)). What humans do hear are perceptual cues: robotic vocals, generic material, and polish. Free-text answers in the 308-person blind test included "Something about Track 1 sounded too perfect, too contrived to be Human" and "Track 2 immediately seemed more human cause it made better use of stereo sound" ([arXiv 2509.25601](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25601)). An instrumental score avoids the vocal cues entirely, so its human feel rests on the next three layers.

### Recorded variation per note is the first realism layer

Realism comes from variation that follows physics. **Velocity layers and round robins solve different problems**: layers change the timbre with dynamic, and round robins give different recordings of the same note ([SampleStack](https://samplestack.app/news/round-robin-explained/)). Staccatos "desperately need" round robins, while for piano dynamic layers matter more ([VI-Control](https://vi-control.net/community/threads/are-round-robins-a-real-advantage-in-a-multi-velocity-layers-piano-library.64111/)). The arithmetic matters. At 150 BPM semiquavers, four round robins cycle back every 0.4 s, inside the window where the ear hears repetition, and 6 to 8 push the return past 1 s. Resetting the cycle on every loop recreates the machine gun ([modwheel.net](https://modwheel.net/guides/why-your-ostinatos-sound-like-a-machine-gun)). The same guide gives a test for whether a patch can deliver that variation: play velocity 40 and 110, and if only loudness changes, velocity is not adding timbral variety. Its fix for too few alternates is to alternate bars between two libraries about 1.5 dB apart through one shared reverb. Sustained lines need a continuous dynamic curve. Bow-change dips of 1 to 3 dB go where a bow would run out (under 1 dB is inaudible, too many sound like "constantly catching its breath"), and dynamics pull back at dissonance and push into resolutions instead of ramping straight ([Composer Workflow](https://agents.composerworkflow.com/academy/humanizing-a-mockup/)). Vibrato in measured violinists runs at a mean of 5 to 7 Hz. Four top soloists averaged 6.63 Hz and 63 cents wide, and a classic measurement puts typical extent near 30 cents within a 10 to 60 cent range ([Illinois](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/a-pilot-study-of-relationships-between-pitch-register-and-dynamic/); [BYU physics](https://physics.byu.edu/docs/publication/1499)). Parts must also be playable. MuseBench lists more than ten simultaneous piano notes, guitar beyond the fretboard and wind lines without breaths as measurable AI tells ([MuseBench, arXiv 2610.20310](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.20310)). And sample quality caps everything: a great performance on a mediocre piano sample still sounds mediocre ([Taxi forum](https://forums.taxi.com/post329840.html)).

For code synthesis the equivalents follow directly, though the exact values are inference rather than sourced numbers. Tie filter cutoff and harmonic count to velocity. Give each note a few cents of random detune and slightly different envelope times. Put a noise component in the attack (breath, bow, pick). Let the release keep ringing after note off. Start vibrato a few hundred milliseconds late with a depth that wanders. These fixes improve a synth. They do not replace a sampled piano, mallet or string, which is why the library section below matters.

### Timing should be systematic, and the pulse should stay tight

The intuitive fix, random timing jitter, is the wrong one. Real players deviate 5 to 50 ms from the grid ([ANU](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstreams/dfcc9a33-70c6-4653-a5e7-1dcc82cb30fe/download)), but the deviations are structured. In Clyde Stubblefield's "Funky Drummer" they shrink from 56 ms to 4 ms across a fill as he returns to the downbeat, and drummers repeat their deviation patterns within a few milliseconds bar to bar ([ZGMTH](https://www.gmth.de/zeitschrift/artikel/1224.aspx)). Listeners rate **perfectly quantized versions as grooving as well as or better than large random deviations** ([Frühauf et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237423294_Music_on_the_timing_grid_The_influence_of_microtiming_on_the_perceived_groove_quality_of_a_simple_drum_pattern_performance); [Senn et al., PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5050221/)), and experts only detect swing displacement at 8 to 14 ms, non-drummers at 18 to 31 ms ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01709/full)). Practitioners therefore put the humanity in velocity and phrasing. For sampled shorts, modwheel.net suggests a 3 to 6 ms offset after quantizing, a velocity spread of at least 20 points that drifts up over four bars and back rather than jittering around a mean, and note lengths varied 10 to 20% in a shape ([modwheel.net](https://modwheel.net/guides/why-your-ostinatos-sound-like-a-machine-gun)). At phrase level only a few notes move: arrival notes at a peak slightly early, notes approaching a cadence slightly late, and ostinatos, arpeggios and trailer percussion left locked ([Composer Workflow](https://agents.composerworkflow.com/academy/humanizing-a-mockup/)). This agrees with the earlier measurement of 129 professional films, where the 22% of onsets off the grid were sound design following the picture, not jittered drums. The working design is a tight pulse, a repeating per-bar accent template, velocity contours, and small pushes of 5 to 15 ms on phrase peaks and cadences. The 3 to 6 ms jitter sits below the swing detection threshold, so it mainly breaks sample identity rather than adding audible looseness.

### Depth comes from early reflections and one shared tail

A dry oscillator mix reads as a game or demo because **front-to-back depth is mostly early reflections and high-frequency roll-off** ([modwheel.net](https://modwheel.net/guides/reverb-for-cinematic-mixing)). Pre-delay is a distance cue: about 30 to 40 ms pulls a source forward and 0 to 8 ms pushes it back ([modwheel.net](https://modwheel.net/guides/basics-of-mixing-cinematic-music)). A quieter horn that stays as dry as the flute reads as a soft instrument at the front, not a distant one (same source). Orchestral mixers build close, mid and far send buses, trim impulse responses to their early reflections, and add one shared algorithmic tail with 40 to 80 ms of pre-delay ([VI-Control](https://vi-control.net/community/threads/what-are-your-techniques-for-early-reflections-on-dry-orchestral-libraries.82511/); [Gearspace](https://gearspace.com/board/music-computers/565702-reverb-settings-orchestral-sample-libraries-share-your-set-ups.html)). When libraries are combined, each one's built-in room is reduced so the shared reverb defines the space ([modwheel.net](https://modwheel.net/guides/why-your-ostinatos-sound-like-a-machine-gun)). The current Bauwerk score gives each bus its own algorithmic Freeverb-style `Reverb` with different room sizes ([audio/ad.py](</home/wopler/dev/bauwerk-video/audio/ad.py>)), which is the "one reverb per track" pattern rather than one room at several depths. A convolution room on two or three send depths, with darker and later reflections for the back instruments, is the cheapest large realism gain available.

Loudness offers no shortcut. Broadcast ads are delivered at -24 LKFS (ATSC A/85) or -23 LUFS (EBU R 128) ([ITU-R BS.2434](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/opb/rep/R-REP-BS.2434-2018-PDF-E.pdf)), and the earlier work established -14 LUFS as the web reference, so a maximized master gains nothing in level and keeps only its flattened dynamics.

## Ad music is written to picture, around one short motif

Working ad composers lock the timing skeleton before they write melody. Jorde Heys spends a 30 s brief "locking down the tempo, any tempo shifts, hit points, and time signature changes" while melodic ideas are still "bubbling away", composes "to and away from" a few moments, and builds the climax "through texture and dynamics": a synth front half, a hard cut before the one line of dialogue, then a "punchier orchestral push" into the message ([AFTRS](https://www.aftrs.edu.au/blog/mastering-the-score-jorde-heys-on-crafting-music-to-brief/)). Keith Kenniff calls his Apple, Google and Toyota work "mini film scores". One Toyota brief asked for spooky ambient in the first half and upbeat pop in the second, as one piece ([NPR / KUER](https://www.kuer.org/2012-08-26/composer-keith-kenniff-keeps-the-ads-humming)). The standard hit point method notes the exact timecode of an event and picks a tempo so a strong beat lands on it ([IRPR](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/glossary/hit-point/)); a textbook commercial cue runs a constant 99 BPM and still lands its hits on downbeats ([Packt](https://packtpub.com/en-mx/product/scoring-to-picture-in-logic-pro-9781837636891/chapter/chapter-10-scoring-a-commercial-14/section/chapter-10-scoring-a-commercial-14)). The formula is BPM = 60 x n / t for a hit at t seconds on beat n. At 60 fps, tempos whose beat is a whole number of frames include 72, 75, 80, 90, 100, 120, 144 and 150 BPM (50, 48, 45, 40, 36, 30, 25 and 24 frames). That matters because the earlier film study found all three mgpro films at 120.2 BPM while only 18% of professional films sit between 117 and 123. Choosing tempo from the cut, not from a default, fixes both problems.

Brand motifs are short and built from few, stable intervals. Intel's five note bong (D flat, D flat, G flat, D flat, A flat, set to "In-tel In-side") uses only unisons, perfect fourths and a fifth and ends open on the fifth ([t3n](https://t3n.de/news/?p=1627039); [Straight Dope](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/intels-sonic-logo/501341)). Antfood's 3 s YouTube logo stacks a diving bass, an upward glissando, a two note figure on the two syllables of "YouTube", a door-unlock texture and a warm **major 7th pad**, designed around contrasts "small to large, close to far, dark to bright" ([Antfood](https://antfood.com/labs/crafting-youtubes-first-sonic-brand)). Memory research explains the shape: across 90 sonic logos, more structural complexity improved recognition but hurt sung recall ([Cambridge repository](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/903c50f3-6d66-4721-af94-7c037073b139)). So the pitches should be simple and the timbre and rhythm distinctive. Mastercard develops one core melody by changing instrument and tempo, not notes, across cinematic, playful and city versions ([Mastercard](https://newsroom.mastercard.com/news/press/2019/february/sound-on-mastercard-debuts-sonic-brand/)). That is the model for a 30 s ad: state the motif softly, fragment it as a counter line, and restate it in its original form at the logo in a brighter register.

Sourced harmonic analysis of ads is thin, but the clichés are named. I-V-vi-IV is "said to be the most used progression in modern music" ([Berklee Online](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/common-chord-progressions-and-how-to-make-them-your-own/)). Corporate stock music follows "the basic composition rules, and only the basic composition rules" ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2019/10/23/how-to-make-bland-soulless-co.html)). The "thoughtful tech" sound descends from Thomas Newman, whose Saving Mr. Banks opening uses only I and IV for sixteen bars with an ostinato carrying the motion ([Film Music Notes](https://filmmusicnotes.com/oscar-nominees-2014-best-original-score-part-2-of-6-thomas-newmans-saving-mr-banks/)), and whose Shawshank theme uses C Dorian with major 7th and minor 9th voicings and half step movement inside chords ([Beato](https://rosetta.to/u/rickbeato/thomas-newman-stoic-theme-shawshank-redemption-breakdown)). That palette of piano, strings and marimba is now itself sold as stock "in the style of Thomas Newman" ([Shockwave-Sound](https://shockwave-sound.com/stock-music-track/14392/distant-horizon-lp)). Lydian's raised fourth gives the "weightless suspension" film composers use for wonder ([Mark Slater](https://markslater.net/unlocking-the-power-of-the-lydian-mode/)). Standard composition practice (not tied to a measured study) turns this into a kit: simple progressions with fresh colour rather than complex progressions. That means a pedal bass under changing upper chords, inversions with added 9ths and sus chords, a Lydian two chord loop (I to II over a tonic pedal) for a product reveal, a single borrowed iv for an "earned" warmth, wide intervals low and close clusters high, and an ending on add9 or maj7 for an open, forward tech feel where Intel and YouTube both end. In 30 s, a register lift usually works better than a key change, and the half or whole step "truck driver" modulation is the most clichéd lift of all.

Arrangement builds energy through texture, not level. Heys does it through palette shifts, and Newman passes an ostinato from piano to strings for a climax ([Scribd notes](https://www.scribd.com/document/487936862/Presentation-Notes)). Professional editors reviewing AI ads named a track that "stays at roughly the same energy level" with no crescendo or drop at the climax as a weakness ([arXiv 2608.24329](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24329)). Trailer practice warns that when every hit is oversized, every riser full band and every cut has a whoosh, the sequence "gets louder but less dramatic", and a held silent frame after a riser often reads bigger than any hit ([add.app](https://add.app/sound-effects/sound-design-for-trailers-hits-rises-drones-pulses/)). Repetition itself is not the problem. Looped random sequences are rated more musical than ones heard once ([University of Arkansas](https://news.uark.edu/articles/22615/repetition-in-music-pulls-us-in-and-pulls-us-together)). Identical audio is the problem: a four or eight bar figure that changes something each pass (a layer, a fill in bar 4, a voicing, a filter opening) reads as arranged. In a 15 to 30 s spot there is room for at most one lift or one drop, with the motif's last note landing on the logo and 1.5 to 3 s of ring kept inside the spot length ([PDX spot assignment](https://web.pdx.edu/~jnewton/assignments/69rs_radio_spot/index.html)).

## A CC0 sample core and headless renderers can replace pure synthesis

Licences narrow the field before quality does. For a paid client commercial the safe order is CC0, then permissive custom licences that name commercial music and do not exclude advertising, then CC BY with a credit. **Two popular free orchestras fail for ads.** Sonatina Symphonic Orchestra uses CC Sampling Plus 1.0, which states that "All advertising and promotional uses are excluded" ([CC legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/sampling+/1.0/legalcode)). Virtual Playing Orchestra embeds Sonatina samples among its sources ([Virtual Playing Orchestra](http://virtualplaying.com/virtual-playing-orchestra/)), so it inherits the problem unless every patch is verified file by file. Spitfire LABS now lives inside Splice INSTRUMENT behind an account ([Splice](https://splice.com/instrument/labs-instrument)) and has no headless Linux route.

For effects, the Sonniss GDC bundles remain the one free source of professional recordings. They are royalty free with "no attribution", explicitly cover "advertisements, trailers", and forbid redistribution as sound effects and use for AI training ([Sonniss licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)). They are also large: the 2024 bundle is 23.5 GB in nine parts ([Sonniss](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc)). One part at a time, keeping only whooshes, impacts, foley, paper, wood and mechanical sounds, fits the disk. Kenney packs are CC0 but game-flavoured ([Kenney](https://kenney.nl/assets/interface-sounds)). Freesound's CC0 subset is reachable through `filter=license:"Creative Commons 0"`, but originals need OAuth2 and the previews are lossy ([Freesound API](https://freesound.org/docs/api/resources_apiv2.html)). Pixabay is no-attribution but custom, with a carve-out for branded content and third-party clearance left to the user ([Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/)). The earlier work adds that a CC0 file is only as clean as its uploader, so each file's source URL, licence version and download date belong in a manifest.

The case for recorded effects over synthesized ones is now experimental. In a 2026 study, 18 audio professionals rated scenes built from library samples above scenes using procedural sounds, by **13.8% (live action) to 34.8% (cartoon everyday scene)**. Synthetic sounds were believable for sci-fi (lasers, rockets, air) and least believable for everyday actions. Classifiers separated the two groups at 90 to 95% using temporal centroid, spectral flatness, high-frequency content, log attack time and effective duration, and the professionals asked for more low end on impacts, a stronger first hit, more layering and more reverb ([arXiv 2605.31082](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31082)). A footstep model that matched the spectrum still lacked "temporal sharpness" until a transient was synthesized explicitly ([arXiv 2412.18710](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.18710)). Layering practice agrees: a transient, a body that carries identity and a tail that gives space, with aligned attacks ([Pixflow](https://pixflow.net/blog/sound-effects-layering/)). So where synthesis remains (UI ticks, sci-fi glows), it needs a sharp transient, a body with a downward pitch envelope, a tail into the shared room, and per-instance variation. Physical events (a card landing, paper, a door, a hand on wood) should be recordings.

On Linux the rendering chain is short. `sfizz_render --sfz X.sfz --midi stem.mid --wav stem.wav -s 48000 -q 3 --use-eot` renders SFZ instruments from MIDI ([sfizz man page](https://github.com/sfztools/sfizz/blob/develop/clients/sfizz_render.man.in)). FluidSynth, already installed, covers SF2 with `fluidsynth -ni -F out.wav -r 48000 font.sf2 song.mid`. Spotify's Pedalboard loads VST3 instruments and effects headless on Linux, so Surge XT (in Arch extra) can play pads from MIDI messages, and its `Convolution` plugin applies the Voxengo rooms ([Pedalboard](https://github.com/spotify/pedalboard); [Surge](https://github.com/surge-synthesizer/surge)). DawDreamer offers the same with Faust and a scheduled MIDI API ([DawDreamer](https://github.com/DBraun/DawDreamer)). The pipeline that follows is: numpy composes, mido writes one MIDI file per stem with humanized velocities, `sfizz_render` and FluidSynth render the stems, and Pedalboard does rooms, bus compression and the limiter. Two things are unverified: whether the Arch `sfizz` package actually ships `sfizz_render` (it may need a split package or a source build), and whether any CC0 felt piano exists. No primary licence for one was found, so felt piano is the one popular ad timbre without a clean free source.

## Contrast must be arranged, because a gain dip sounds like a fault

The dropouts have a measurable explanation. The just noticeable level change is about 1 dB under good conditions ([HyperPhysics](https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/Sound/db.html)), most listeners need about 3 dB ([Digital Sound and Music](https://digitalsoundandmusic.com/chapters/ch4/4-2)), and **10 dB sounds about half as loud** ([Sengpielaudio](https://www.sengpielaudio.com/calculator-loudness.htm)). A dip under 3 dB therefore buys no contrast, and a 6 to 10 dB dip is clearly heard, so it needs a reason the ear can find: a cadence, a picture event, an arrangement change. A level drop with no musical cause gives the ear nothing to explain it, and it reads as a fault. Gaps of only 4 to 5 ms are audible in continuous sound, and detection gets up to ten times worse when the sounds either side of the gap differ in spectrum ([Phillips et al. 1997](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/9193057)). That explains why professionals "keep a bed": a pad, room tone or reverb tail under the gap fills the spectrum, so the ear hears a change of texture, not a hole. Production guides say parts should leave "without it sounding like they were suddenly muted" ([Arends notes](https://publish.obsidian.md/arends-digital-garden/Notes/20+Shareable/22+Music+Production+%F0%9F%94%88/22.03+Recording+Tips/How+to+create+transitions+in+your+arrangements)), and spoken-audio practice holds that about one second of silence already feels like an uncomfortable gap ([Radio Today](https://radiotoday.com.au/silence-is-golden-why-shutting-up-makes-people-listen/)).

This corrects how the earlier film measurements were applied. They found that 83% of professional films contain a 20 dB dip of at least 150 ms and that re-entry dips last a median 0.3 s before the sound returns about 9 dB louder. Those numbers describe the output of arrangement (everything stops on a cadence while a tail rings, then the full band re-enters on a cut). They are not instructions to automate gain. The current Bauwerk score code does the second thing. It multiplies the whole music bus to exactly zero for 0.25 s before the drop and for 0.5 s before the payoff, with 4 ms fades, **after** the bus reverbs, so every tail is cut along with the notes. It also ducks the tonal buses by 45% (about 5 dB) and the bass by about 7.6 dB on every kick, recovering over roughly 110 ms, and steps the music level between plateaus (0.68 to 0.5 at 6 s, about 2.7 dB) ([audio/ad.py](</home/wopler/dev/bauwerk-video/audio/ad.py>)). By the evidence above, each gesture is off grid in cause if not in time, tail-less, or both. An intended breath has three signs: it lands on the grid right after a cadence or a hit, it keeps a tail or a bed, and it resolves into something bigger within one beat to one bar (0.5 to 2 s at 120 BPM). True silence is safest as a short 100 to 300 ms gap immediately before a hit, where the hit arrives before the ear can wonder. That range is an inference from the one-beat gap advice ([FaderPro](https://blog.faderpro.com/creativity/silence-in-music-production/)), not a measured threshold, and no study was found on when audiences read a level dip as intended.

### Transitions sit on the grid and carry one sound across the cut

Musical transitions have conventional lengths. Fills run "typically less than a bar" and break up a section without carrying a large change ([Arends notes](https://publish.obsidian.md/arends-digital-garden/Notes/20+Shareable/22+Music+Production+%F0%9F%94%88/22.03+Recording+Tips/How+to+create+transitions+in+your+arrangements)). Risers span one to several bars, and their length sets their slope ([UJAM](https://www.ujam.com/tutorials/how-to-create-huge-edm-transitions/)). At 120 BPM a bar is 2 s, so a four bar riser would eat a third of a 30 s spot; short ads need one to two bar builds. Film editors bridge scenes with J cuts (incoming audio before the picture) and L cuts (outgoing audio after it), finding the pacing on a straight cut first and then splitting ([Boris FX](https://borisfx.com/blog/l-and-j-cuts-smoother-more-cinematic/)). The strongest bridge is Randy Thom's **metamorphosis**: a mill's clank that becomes a train's click-clack, because when a sound changes in response to the story "its power and richness grow exponentially" ([Randy Thom](http://filmsound.org/articles/designing_for_sound.htm)). Whooshes should match the length of the movement and peak just before the cut ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/fr/resources/sounds/whoosh-sound-effect)), but not every cut needs one. Whooshes on every cut make a film "feel like a template instead of a story" ([add.app](https://add.app/sound-effects/sound-design-for-trailers-hits-rises-drones-pulses/)). The braam is the decade's most overused trailer sound, and editors swap it for a subfall or "downer". Pitched risers and braams must match the score's key or "the audience senses something is off" ([Duende Sounds](https://duendesounds.com/trailer-sound-design-elements/)).

Motion and sound should share one curve. Apple's audio-haptic principles are causality, harmony and utility: it must be obvious what caused a sound, and sound and picture must agree as they would in the real world ([Apple WWDC19](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/810)). Practitioners say the sound "needs to breathe with that curve, not fire a transient at the start and stop" ([Cursa](https://cursa.app/en/page/audio-and-final-rhythm-using-sound-to-support-motion)), and Boom Box Post's Jeff Shiffman notes that "moving a sound effect only a few frames can change a moment from flat to outright hilarious" ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/animation-sound-design/)). No source maps easing to envelopes formally. The derivable rule is to drive gain, cutoff and pitch from the same per-frame velocity as the motion: an ease-in move swells into a transient at the end, an ease-out move attacks fast and decays with the deceleration, an overshoot gets a second small blip, and a damped spring gets repeats spaced at its own period.

### Feeling follows arousal more reliably than brand adjectives

Psychology supports a two-axis map. Tempo is "typically regarded as the most important" factor, loudness and pitch variation raise arousal, mode and consonance shift valence, legato reads calm or cohesive and staccato reads tense, energetic or playful ([Wikipedia, Music and emotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_and_emotion)). A 2025 meta-analysis found arousal predicted better (r = 0.81) than valence (r = 0.67) ([C4DM, Eerola](https://www.c4dm.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/news/2025-06-06.C4DM-Seminar_Tuomas_Eerola)). Juslin's mechanisms give an ad its levers: a brain stem reflex for sudden loud hits, rhythmic entrainment for pulse, musical expectancy for tension and release, and conditioning for a sonic logo ([Juslin 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00596/full)). Brand words such as "premium", "trust" or "precision" have no peer-reviewed mapping. Their translation into sound (premium as restraint, fewer elements, longer tails and one well made signature sound, which is how Netflix layered a real knock under a musical tail) is practitioner judgment, and Thom's remark that "we interpret sound with our emotions, not our intellect" is the honest summary ([Randy Thom](http://filmsound.org/articles/designing_for_sound.htm)).

## A logo should arrive as one object, never as two images

The best documented resolves treat the logo as something that already moves, so the film comes to rest in its motion rather than adding a treatment at the end. Google's 2015 dots are "a dynamic and perpetually moving state of the logo" that move "along geometric arcs" with "a standard set of snappy easing curves" ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/evolving-google-identity/)), and animation was "almost the first part of the design process" ([Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2015/11/09/interview-motion-design-and-the-google-identity/)). Meta's symbol was drawn as one continuous line in VR and designed "to live in motion and 3D" ([Meta Design](https://www.meta.com/design-at-meta/blog/designing-our-new-company-brand-meta/)). Netflix's 2019 ident flies the camera into the N to reveal a spectrum built from show thumbnails ([Creative Review](https://www.creativereview.co.uk/netflix-updates-its-ident/)). Mastercard hides its two circles in parachutes, drums and sun hats ([Digital Synopsis](https://digitalsynopsis.com/advertising/mastercard-believe-in-experiences/)). Dropbox rebuilt its box as flat diamonds that play against the old 3D reading ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/2-8-october-2017/dropbox-rebrands-show-just-file-storage/)). ManvsMachine assembled More4's mark from triangles flipping like pages ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/manvsmachine-more-4-identity)). Five patterns cover the cases: a motion state resolving into the mark, a single line drawing it, a camera dive into it, the mark hidden in real objects, and assembly from its own parts.

What makes these premium is documented in principle more than in frames. Google's Jonathan Jarvis calls restraint the second biggest challenge, with "a pretty messy cutting room floor", and wants motion "fast, simple, and clear" ([Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2015/11/09/interview-motion-design-and-the-google-identity/)). Microsoft's Fluent gives larger elements more time and keeps linear motion for constant-rate rotations only ([Fluent 2](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/motion)). Arrivals decelerate: WinUI's entrance curve is cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0, 1) ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/motion/timing-and-easing)), Material's emphasized decelerate is (0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1.0) ([Flutter](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/Easing-class.html)), and Apple reduces springs to duration and bounce, with examples at 0.6 s and bounce 0.2 ([WWDC23](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2023/10158/)). Toyota's end frame rules are the most concrete handoff spec found. The last shot is art directed for the transition, wide shots are preferred over centred key objects, story action must conclude before the logo transition starts, there must be no cuts during it, and **"There should always be at least one clean frame between the graphics and the logo animation"** ([Toyota NZ](https://brandguidelines.toyota.co.nz/tvc-endframe)). Hold times are set by guidelines: the Ad Council requires a logo on screen at least 3 s on :15 spots and 5 s on :20 and longer ([Ad Council](https://www.adcouncil.org/media-standards-guide/television)), and a government style guide asks 1.5 s on 15 s ads and 2.5 s on 30 s ads ([Tas Gov](https://www.communications.tas.gov.au/styleguide/p_-_television/30_seconds_and_above)). Apple films hold the product name about 1 to 2 s and then the logo about 2 s (own measurement in the research notes, [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNhX1uR2vO8)). The sound mirrors the arc: Netflix's brief was "tension and release" in one sound, a real knock with a reversed swelling tail ([MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/news/the-netflix-ta-dum-sound-features-a-reversed-electric-guitar-sample-that-was-created-using-90s-effect-pedals)), and the accent sits exactly where the animation completes ([Cursa](https://cursa.app/en/page/audio-and-final-rhythm-using-sound-to-support-motion)).

Cheapness is also documented. School of Motion bought the same logo animation for $7, $150 and $1,000, and **84.5% of more than 100 blind viewers chose the professional one**. The cheap versions were "sparkle engineering. Very standard and stiff" and "cluttered" with "things moving everywhere", while the professional one "fit the concept of our brand" ([School of Motion](https://www.schoolofmotion.com/blog/motion-design-price-difference)). Critics tell motion designers to remove effects that look "just dropped on top rather than a necessary part of the animation", to draw scaffolding lines rather than fade them ([r/MotionDesign mirror](https://redlib.ssps.io/r/MotionDesign/comments/1lkcfbj/logo_design_and_animation_for_a_client)), and complain when letters are "washed over" by an effect instead of built by it ([r/animation mirror](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/animation/comments/1igo0gs/what_do_you_think_of_this_animated_logo_im)). Morphs with poor point correspondence "turn inside out" even when point counts match, which is why GSAP exposes a `shapeIndex` to choose the mapping ([GSAP MorphSVG](https://gsap.com/community/morphSVG)). Packshots fail when the object changes shape during a push in or the logo softens as the light moves ([AI Film Contests](https://aifilmcontests.com/guide/ai-commercial-packshot-and-end-card)). And motion cannot rescue a generic form: nearly 1,200 earlier logos used Meta's infinity loop ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2021/11/03/meta-facebook-rebrand-infinity-loop/)).

A transparent morph from a house into a cube belongs to the same family as fading scaffolding and washing letters over. While both are semi-transparent, the viewer sees two objects at once, and the internal edges of each show through the other. The eye reads an edit, not a transformation. No source names "x-ray" crossfades as a failure in those words; this is an extension of the documented critiques. An isometric cube is the easy case, though, because its silhouette is a hexagon and its visible faces are three rhombi. The options below follow from the documented patterns; none is a published cube case study.

| Approach | How it works for house to isometric cube | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Match cut on the isometric axis | Move the 3D camera to the true isometric view (equal foreshortening on three edges) as the house settles, scale and centre it so its massing fills the cube's hexagon, cut on one clean frame to the flat mark | Hard cut on a downbeat, the button on the first frame of the mark |
| Collapse into its own faces | Fade nothing. Roof, walls and floor slabs fold or slide as opaque planes into the three rhombi, each face keeping a constant tone through the move (like Dropbox's flat faces) | One material sound (wood, paper fold) that morphs into the motif's last note, Thom style |
| Camera dive | Fly into one corner of the building until a face fills frame, then pull out to reveal it is the cube's face | Riser of one to two bars, a short suck back, the button on the reveal |
| Single line draws it | The building's outline or the cursor's last stroke becomes the cube's edges as a trim path, decelerating into closure | Accent on the frame the stroke closes |

All four share the rules the sources support: the story action ends first, one clean frame separates any UI or text from the logo move, the move is a single decisive motion on a strong decelerate or a low-bounce spring (about 0.6 s, bounce about 0.2), there is no secondary sparkle, the sonic accent lands on the landing frame (or one frame early), and the still mark holds about 1.5 to 3 s.

## Pace by visible change every 1 to 3 seconds, not by cuts

There is no published cut-rate study of tech launch films, so the research measured 17 with ffmpeg scene detection. Three pacing families appear. **Hardware hero films cut every 2 to 4 s**: Apple's iPhone 17 Pro film has an average shot of 2.3 s and a median of 1.6 s ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TNhX1uR2vO8)), MacBook Pro 3.7 s ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JlS6RmAJKnE)), Samsung's S24 design film 3.5 s ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ePdbj2bZ-Ro)). **Feature parades cut every 1.2 to 1.6 s**: Figma's Config 2024 film has 58 cuts in 80 s and 8 in its first 5 s ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0wCIP2Ox-EQ)), and Arc Max averages 1.2 s ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ttylMKwIe7c)). **SaaS UI films barely cut at all**: Linear's Releases film is one continuous camera drift over a release graph ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6dIwFoQ0eVg)), Framer holds one shot for 21 s ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6xJWQRVl9nw)), and Raycast averages 4.8 s ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mi173xGb0ZA)). For comparison, Hollywood fell below 4 s per shot after 2000, with shorter shots carrying more motion ([Cutting et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3485803/)), and social "cinematic ads" average about 1.2 s ([arXiv 2608.24329](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24329)). In the slow family, pace comes from change inside the shot. Holds of 9 to 19 s in Apple and Samsung films occur only on continuous camera moves, and the Pixel 9 Pro intro survives 20 s of one shot because the typed prompt keeps growing ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OMVpP-Zam1A)). A practical target follows: some visible change every 1 to 3 s (a cut, a new element, a camera direction change, a UI state change), even inside a long take.

The other limit is comprehension. Netflix caps adult reading at 20 characters per second with a 0.83 s minimum event ([Netflix](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)). Viewers grasp a picture's gist in 13 ms but need about 500 ms of processing to remember it ([Potter 2012](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00032/text)), and brand memory builds at roughly 1.5 to 2.5 s of active attention ([Advertising Week](https://advertisingweek.com/the-second-second-is-the-moment-that-matters-most-for-brands-right-now/); [Creative Salon](https://creative.salon/articles/features/vccp-media-dr-karen-nelson-field-hacking-the-attention-economy)). Fast cutting raises arousal, attention and memory ([Lang 2000](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/the-effects-of-edits-on-arousal-attention-and-memory-for-televisi/)), but verbal recognition suffers first when pace and arousing content combine, so a 1 s cut is fine for pure picture and too fast for a shot that also carries words. These figures give working holds, derived rather than measured. Text gets about max(0.9 s, characters / 15) plus 0.5 s to settle, so a three word headline needs about 1.7 s and a seven word line about 3 s. A UI change gets about 1.5 to 2.5 s (land the eye, show the change, hold the result). The logo gets 1.5 to 2.5 s or more.

"Slow" usually means waiting on a process. Editors compress with elliptical cuts that skip the middle of an action ([Filmdaft](https://filmdaft.com/elliptical-editing-how-films-compress-time-into-seconds/)), jump cuts with speed ramps, and staggers of 0.1 to 0.2 s that turn many entrances into one overlapping sequence ([Webflow](https://university.webflow.com/course-lesson/interactions-animations-sequencing-timing)). Editors reviewing AI ads flagged two traps: near-identical consecutive framings that stutter, and taglines arriving at "a very awkward point" so viewers "don't know what this ad is for until the end" ([arXiv 2608.24329](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24329)). For an AI or build process, the derived recipe is to show the trigger in full (about 1 s), compress the middle to two to four jumps of 0.4 to 0.8 s or one shot ramped at 3 to 8x, then hold the result 1.5 to 2.5 s. Overlap each action's start with the previous one's end by 0.1 to 0.3 s, so there is never a frame of dead waiting, and cut on action rather than after motion settles.

The opening decides the outcome. Viewers make a go or no-go decision within 2 to 3 s ([RBR](https://rbr.com/is-the-power-of-06-ads-a-concern-for-broadcast-tv/)). People who watched under 3 s produced up to 47% of campaign value in Facebook and Nielsen's study ([MarTech](https://martech.org/even-brief-video-views-drive-brand-lift-facebook-nielsen-study-finds/)). Recognisable brand assets in the first 2 s lifted recall in System1's 887-ad TikTok study, while plain logo overlays reduced it ("They recognize the brand but scroll along") ([Chief Marketer](https://chiefmarketer.com/system1-tiktok-study-branding-elements-and-creator-led-content-improve-short-form-video-ads/)). Wistia's tail data says viewers leave once they sense the value is over, so recaps hurt and end logos should stay short ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/understanding-audience-retention)). The measured endings agree: Apple runs about 1 to 2 s of product name plus about 2 s of logo, Notion about 6 s across CTA, tagline and logo, Arc three 1 s cards ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S92KX8-Hmlc); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ttylMKwIe7c)). A 30 s structure consistent with all of this:

| Time | Job | Picture | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2 s | Hook | The product or its effect, with a few words, not a logo intro | One texture, the motif fragment, below full level |
| 2 to 22 s | Three to five beats of 4 to 6 s | Each beat is two to four shots or UI changes, a visible change every 1 to 3 s | Layers and register added at the turns, one breath at most |
| 22 to 27 s | Payoff | The single most impressive moment | The peak, on the payoff |
| 27 to 30 s | Name and logo | One clean frame, one move, 1.5 to 3 s hold | The button on the landing frame, tail rings out |

Length is a placement decision. Kantar found 15 s spots "can hit just as hard as 30s" on YouTube and Instagram while 30 s did better on linear TV ([Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/company-news/kantar-super-bowl-ad-study-15-second-ads-match-30-second-spots)), and TikTok saw its best conversion at 21 to 34 s ([Mobile Marketing Reads](https://mobilemarketingreads.com/?p=20237)). Launch films where people chose to watch run 30 to 60 s at Apple, Linear and Notion.

## Cursors read as human when timing follows distance and clicks have weight

Every serious screen recorder converges on the same kit, and open source code supplies the numbers that Screen Studio does not publish. Cap, which describes itself as inspired by Screen Studio, smooths the cursor with a spring at **tension 470, mass 3, friction 70**, which trails its target by about 149 ms and is nearly critically damped (zeta about 0.93). It switches to a stiffer click spring (530, 1, 40) within 175 ms of a click and looks ahead 500 ms so the cursor arrives on the target ([Cap cursor_interpolation.rs](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/main/crates/rendering/src/cursor_interpolation.rs)). A click shrinks the cursor to 0.8 over 130 ms with smoothstep, starting before mouse down ([Cap cursor.rs](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/main/crates/rendering/src/layers/cursor.rs)). Tilt is horizontal pixels travelled in the last 0.4 s x 0.03 x 0.15, clamped to 20 degrees, which gives only about 4.5 degrees for a 1000 px move ([Cap lib.rs](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/main/crates/rendering/src/lib.rs)). Motion blur is a directional smear proportional to per-frame travel and normalized to frame rate (same source as the click). The camera spring is heavier than the cursor's (stiffness 200, damping 40, mass 2.25) ([Cap configuration.rs](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/main/crates/project/src/configuration.rs)). Remotion's `spring()` takes the same stiffness, damping and mass, so these values transfer directly, though the integration should be checked. Tella's product demo preset uses a 2.5x cursor with tilt, click spring and ripple on, a smaller 1.5x cursor in deep zooms so it does not hide labels, and it lists "no visible reaction on clicks" as a mistake ([Tella](https://www.tella.com/blog/animated-cursors-for-screen-recordings)).

Human pointing gives the motion model. The ballistic part of a reach follows a minimum jerk profile whose speed peaks mid-move at 1.875 times the average ([Duarte notebook](https://nbviewer.jupyter.org/github/demotu/BMC/blob/master/notebooks/MinimumJerkHypothesis.ipynb)). But it fits real mouse pointing "very poorly" as a whole, because real moves are a quick primary movement, often undershooting, followed by corrections ([arXiv 2002.11596](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.11596); [J NeuroEng Rehab](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1186/1743-0003-5-28)). Duration grows with log distance over target size (Fitts), and target size lengthens the corrective phase while distance only scales the curve ([MacKenzie](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/hfes2009.html)). Films keep the bell-shaped speed and the slight curve and drop the jitter. Peekaboo's engineering model uses 280 to 350 ms under 200 px, 400 to 900 ms for 200 to 800 px and 900 to 1700 ms beyond, on a shallow Bezier arc, with overshoot near zero for short hops and about 20% for long ones ([Peekaboo](https://github.com/steipete/Peekaboo/blob/main/docs/human-mouse-move.md)). HeyGen's launch-video house style travels 0.4 to 0.92 s on power3.out, lands the arrow tip (not the box centre) on the target, presses to 0.84 in 0.1 s and releases over 0.22 s, scales the clicked button to 0.94 in the same frames, and fires the next beat on the click frame. It sizes the full-frame cursor at about 7% of frame width ("never smaller"), enters from below the frame, exits by accelerating off an edge in 0.5 to 0.7 s, never fades in or out in place, and in the last 0.3 s before a hard cut accelerates toward the next scene's first click point so the next shot continues at matching speed ([HyperFrames](https://skills.cat/skills/heygen-com/hyperframes/oversized-cursor)). Animade's Figma campaign shows the ceiling: the "simple triangle that is usually overlooked" got personality through "motion, weight and timing" ([Animade](https://archive.animade.tv/work/figma)).

| Parameter | Value to use | Source |
|---|---|---|

The documented mistakes are a tiny cursor, jitter and no zoom ([Two Average Gamers](https://www.twoaveragegamers.com/how-i-make-product-demos-that-dont-look-like-garbage/)), linear constant-speed motion ([Liveblocks](https://liveblocks.io/blog/how-to-animate-multiplayer-cursors)), fades in place and a cursor frozen on the action ([HyperFrames](https://skills.cat/skills/heygen-com/hyperframes/oversized-cursor)), and rapid cursor-shape swaps that tools filter out ([Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/guide/cursor)). The strongest robotic tell is identical timing: every move with the same duration, curve and ease. The current Bauwerk cursor shows several of these ([src/ad/Cursor.tsx](</home/wopler/dev/bauwerk-video/src/ad/Cursor.tsx>)). Visibility fades in and out in place over 8 frames. Every move uses the same symmetric minimum jerk ease with a default arc of 0.1 on the same side. The press is 3 frames down and 9 up at 60 fps (50 and 150 ms) with a 14% shrink, and nothing on the target reacts. Speed is shown by two ghost copies at 18% and 8% opacity rather than a directional smear. The 96 px image at scale 0.74 is about 71 px, roughly half the 7% guideline at 1920 px. ## Conclusion

The four criticisms of the last film turn out to be one mistake made four times: **a change shown as a blend between two states instead of as one thing transforming for a visible reason**. A music bus multiplied to zero is the audio version of an opacity crossfade. The house fading through the cube is the visual version. A cursor that fades in where it already is, rather than arriving from somewhere, is the interaction version. A synth note with no attack noise, no drift and no room is a tone that exists rather than one that was played. In each case the professional alternative keeps a single continuous object (a ringing tail under the breath, opaque faces folding into the mark, a cursor entering from off frame) and lets its change have a cause the viewer can read: a cadence, a camera axis, a click. This gives the pipeline a check that works across disciplines. Wherever code interpolates gain or opacity between two states, ask what physical or musical event explains the change, and if none does, replace the interpolation with an arrangement or a transformation.

The second lesson concerns how measurements become rules. The earlier film study correctly found that professionals make contrast with short dips, but translating "20 dB for 0.3 s" into a gain automation reproduced the number and lost the cause, and the result read as a dropout. Output metrics are symptoms of craft decisions. They are useful for checking a render, and harmful when used as the instruction that generates one. The same caution applies to every number in this report, from 6 to 8 round robins to a 149 ms cursor lag. The order of work that follows: move instruments and physical sounds onto CC0 samples in one convolution room, write the score from the cut's hit points with a motif that ends on the logo frame, replace every hard gain cut with an arranged breath, rebuild the end as one opaque transformation with a clean frame and a held mark, and give the cursor distance-based timing and an asymmetric ease. Then measure the new film against the professional set to confirm that the right numbers moved for the right reasons.
