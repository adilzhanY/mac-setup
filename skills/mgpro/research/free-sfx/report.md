# Rotated takes and restraint make stock sounds human

Editors who cut product ads, explainers and motion graphics with free sound effects do three things that a code pipeline can copy. They draw from a short list of libraries whose licences survive a paid client job, they commit to a palette of three or four sound families per film, and they spend their effort on choosing, trimming and varying a few sounds rather than on adding more. About a dozen free sources allow commercial and client use with no credit: **Kenney (CC0), the Sonniss GDC bundles, Mixkit, Pixabay, 99Sounds, Freesound filtered to CC0, BOOM's free packs, Soundly Free, SoundJay, SoundGator and a few small whoosh packs**, plus Zapsplat once you pay for Premium. Zapsplat's free tier, Freesound CC BY and most itch.io UI packs need a credit, which a 15 second ad has no room for. BBC RemArc, Freesound CC BY-NC and Sampling+, and the YouTube Audio Library outside YouTube are unusable for client ads. Measurements of real films set the targets. Ten published SaaS and brand films carry only **0 to 9 clearly designed accents per minute, 7 to 13 dB over the music bed**, mostly risers, drops, a few whooshes and a logo hit. Two sound-designed launch films with published stems carry three continuous families sitting 3 to 14 dB under the music, rising above it only in the music's gaps. Audible UI clicks were rare, and where present they were tied to one meaningful action. That supports the pipeline owner's first note: the clicks that drive the story must be clearly heard (about 6 to 9 dB over the bed, high-passed and bright, with a brief 2 to 4 dB music dip), so there can only be a few of them, and the rest of the interface stays quiet or silent. It also supports the second note: **the most cited amateur tell is the same whoosh on every move**, and the cure is a pool of 5 to 8 takes per sound type, never the same file twice in a row, each trimmed to the length of its move, with a fresh pool for each film because the earlier AI films reused 42% of their strong transients across films.

### Excluded or unverified for client ads

BBC Sound Effects is the clearest exclusion: "Commercial use of this content is not allowed under the RemArc license", and the licence adds "Don't associate our content with advertising or sponsorship" ([BBC](https://sound-effects.bbcrewind.co.uk/licensing)). Freesound CC BY-NC and Sampling+ files, Orange Free Sounds items marked CC BY-NC ([Orange Free Sounds](https://orangefreesounds.com/futuristic-sci-fi-whoosh-transition-sound-effect/)), SoundBible's non-commercial files ([SoundBible](https://soundbible.com/about.php)) and the popular "Sound Pack for Motion Designers", which is CC BY-NC 4.0 even though it is sold to motion designers ([Gumroad](https://vincentschwenk.gumroad.com/l/GnyNM)), are all out. The YouTube Audio Library is "copyright-safe" on YouTube and will not be claimed through Content ID there ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en)), but its use elsewhere is not stated, so it does not suit an ad that runs on several platforms. Partners In Rhyme says its free sounds "are not owned by Partners In Rhyme" ([PIR](https://www.partnersinrhyme.com/pir/PIRsfx.shtml)). Videvo now redirects to Freepik with terms that could not be read, and the free tiers of Motion Array, Envato, Pond5 and Glitchmachines could not be confirmed. The Gumroad editor packs (Editors Den's 99 cinematic effects, the urfasteditor "1000+" pack with 4,877 ratings, rawAmbience's 10 whooshes) promise "no copyright issues" but publish no licence and credit no sound designer ([Editors Den](https://editorsden.gumroad.com/l/gjwjk); [urfasteditor](https://urfasteditor.gumroad.com/l/sounds)), so their claim cannot be checked. A subtler trap sits in developer tools: soundcn ships 700+ UI sounds that are mostly Kenney CC0, but its 110 World of Warcraft sounds are "property of Blizzard Entertainment" for non-commercial use only ([soundcn README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kapishdima/soundcn/main/README.md)).

### Four clauses matter more than "free"

Four details decide what a pipeline can do with these files. First, **a stock sound may never become a client's sonic logo**: Pixabay bars use "as part of a trade-mark", Adobe bars audio in "logos or ... branding material" and CapCut bars use "as a ... logo", and the Content ID bans at Mixkit and Soundsnap point the same way ([Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/); [Adobe](https://stock.adobe.com/license-terms); [Soundsnap](https://www.soundsnap.com/licence)). Second, **raw files cannot ship inside a template or a shared repository** unless they are CC0: Mixkit, Sonniss, 99Sounds and Adobe all forbid it. Third, **CC0 and user-upload sites carry no provenance promise**. A Pixabay ambience reportedly failed Epic's content review ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/how-can-i-prove-its-royalty-free-sound/1251253)), and only Sonniss gives a written non-infringement warranty, so curated libraries (Sonniss, Kenney, Mixkit, 99Sounds) are safer than random uploads for client work. Fourth, **Content ID risk for short effects is low**: the only registrations found concern music tracks (Pixabay's shield icon), and Uppbeat says YouTube's system targets music rather than effects ([Pixabay FAQ](https://pixabay.com/service/faq/); [Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/sfx)). The real exposure is a re-uploaded commercial sound, not fingerprinting. Mixkit and Pixabay never mention clients by name, so their client cover rests on the general commercial grant; 99Sounds and Zapsplat are the only free licences that name client work outright.

## Editors search with three words and audition at matched loudness

Real searches are short and generic, which is why results sound alike. Freesound's most searched terms in 2024 were "wind, rain, explosion, music, whoosh, footsteps, woosh, click", and **over 81% of searches kept the default relevance sort** ([Freesound blog](https://blog.freesound.org/?p=2141)). Everyone who types "whoosh" therefore downloads the same few files: qubodup's CC0 "Whoosh" (ID 60013) has about 209,800 downloads ([Freesound search](https://freesound.org/search/?q=whoosh&f=license%3A%22Creative+Commons+0%22&s=Downloads+(most+first))). It is a fine sound and also the most heard whoosh on the free internet. Freesound files average 73 seconds ([Freesound blog](https://blog.freesound.org/?p=2141)), so the useful moment usually sits inside a long recording and has to be cut out.

Pixabay's own guidance gives the better query pattern: **an action word plus a feel word plus a context word** ("whoosh soft short", "impact deep muted", "UI click success", "typing modern"), every synonym (whoosh, swoosh, swish, woosh; impact, hit, slam; bubble, pop, plop), and a duration filter of **under 1 s for UI and 1 to 3 s for transitions** ([Pixabay blog](https://pixabay.com/blog/posts/free-and-high-quality-sound-effects-for-video-edit-453/)). Freesound supports including and excluding words ("+water -rain") and duration limits in advanced search ([Freesound forum](https://freesound.org/forum/production-techniques-music-gear-tips-and-tricks/44539)), and its API takes filters such as `license:"Creative Commons 0"`, `duration:[0.0 TO 1.0]` and `avg_rating:[4.5 TO 5.0]`, with the singular field name `tag` ([Creative Commons](https://opensource.creativecommons.org/blog/entries/freesound-intro/); [Freesound API](https://freesound.org/docs/api/resources_apiv2.html)). Three cheap tricks get sounds other editors have not used: sort by newest instead of relevance, add a material word ("cloth", "paper", "air", "bamboo", "stick", "flag"), and exclude the trailer vocabulary ("-cinematic -trailer -epic"). The filter order that follows is licence first (CC0), then duration, then WAV at 48 kHz, which Pixabay calls "a safe default for video" ([Pixabay blog](https://pixabay.com/blog/posts/free-and-high-quality-sound-effects-for-video-edit-453/)).

Choosing between twenty similar whooshes follows one rule: **the sound follows the motion's "speed, mass, direction, distance, and stopping point"** ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide)). Short, bright envelopes read fast and light, long low ones read big and heavy; MKBHD's team gives a rotating phone "a big, low-pitched whoosh to convey its mass" ([Gistly summary](https://gist.ly/youtube-summarizer/master-video-editing-creative-choices-pro-tips)). Sonilo's audition advice is to **level-match the shortlist before judging**, because the louder take always sounds better, then drop each candidate with its peak on the fastest frame and nudge it one frame either way at normal speed ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide)). Pitched sounds must match the music's key or be unpitched ([CRI Middleware](https://blog.criware.com/index.php/2020/05/20/keeping-sfx-in-key/)). Before any of this, editors commit to a style: Herman Huang's rule is "commit to a style instead of mixing random sounds", since gritty bass hits do not belong in a corporate film ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tzfzkTAQsnE)).

A few sounds announce a template on their own. The Vine boom, a 2012 library sound that peaked on TikTok and YouTube in 2020 to 2022, still signals meme editing ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/vine-boom-sound-effect)), and Hans Zimmer said he felt "horrible" hearing Inception-style braams in trailers ([The Playlist](https://theplaylist.net/hans-zimmer-feels-horrible-when-his-inception-bramms-are-used-in-movie-trailers-20131106)). For business content Videotto advises sticking "to whooshes, pops and clean zooms" ([Videotto](https://videotto.com/blog/add-sound-effects-and-meme-edits)). OS system sounds (macOS Tink, the iPhone keyboard tick) are both recognisable and bundled with commercial operating systems, so they should be avoided; that licence point is an inference, not a checked finding. The bigger risk in product work is the default top-of-search whoosh reused on every move, which media.io names directly: edits feel cheap "when the same type of sweep is used again and again" ([media.io](https://www.media.io/audio-effects/whoosh-sound-effects.html)).

Library habits make the rest possible. Working editors keep folders shallow (Transitions, UI, Ambience, Foley), process an inbox folder weekly, and rename files on ingest ([Wideframe](https://try-wideframe.pages.dev/blog/how-to-organize-sound-effects-and-music-for-creator-projects)), with professional libraries using the UCS pattern CatID_FXName_CreatorID_SourceID ([Spencer Bruce](https://www.spencerbruce.com/blog1/2025/10/3/understanding-the-universal-category-system-ucs)). One vendor puts a solid library at 50 to 100 whoosh variations across short, long, light, heavy and stylised ([D Jordan Media](https://djordanmedia.com/blogs/news/best-sound-effects-for-video-editing)). With nearly half of new Freesound uploads needing a credit, keeping source URL, author, licence and download date beside each file is the only way to produce credits or defend a claim later.

## Published product films use few loud accents and many quiet layers

Two different practices exist, and both matter for the per-type numbers below. **Sound-designed launch films** carry dense, continuous effect layers. Tomas Havranek published separated stems for the Contra and HeroUI launch films, and measuring them shows three families each, audible 28 to 61% of the running time ([Contra](https://contra.com/p/WPMQbb6O-sound-design-exploration-contra); [HeroUI](https://contra.com/p/ks662MrZ-hero-ui-launch-video-sound-design-exploration)). In the 30 s Contra film, transitions (long low swells, not discrete whooshes) sat a median **2.6 dB under the music**, the typewriter thread 7.5 dB under and the digital UI tones 13.8 dB under. The only effects that jumped 17 to 21 dB above the music fell in silent gaps between the music's hits, and once the music stopped completely for 2.4 s so the effects carried the moment alone. HeroUI's whooshes were 0.9 to 4 s noise swells, about one every 3 s, and its impacts and risers stem was the loudest family, about as loud as the whole mix. Counted as placed gestures the two films have 46 to 54 per minute; counted as onsets, 217 to 242, because typing runs and ticking patterns hold many onsets inside one gesture.

**Published SaaS and brand films** lean on music cut to picture plus a handful of structural accents. Across ten films measured from the mix (Superhuman, Linear Releases, Arc 1.0, Framer, Screen Studio, Supademo, a Zelios agency explainer, What a Story, Motion Swell, Raycast Pro), the clearly designed effects numbered **0 to 9 per minute**: risers into drops, impacts on section starts and logos, a whoosh on the switch from live action to the UI, and silence or a hard cut before a hit ([Superhuman](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4XKq7qGnJus); [Raycast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u6iro1jHujs); [Screen Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7AnqbpeJoHc)). Accents peaked **7 to 13 dB over the bed** (the median level of the 1.5 s before them) in the middle of a film, and 17 to 25 dB only after a quiet intro or a silence. Raycast's end is the template: a 1.45 s noise riser at +9.2 dB, a 150 ms cut to silence, then the logo impact at +12.8 dB over the bed. Across all ten films whooshes appeared at only 4 to 5 transitions in total, never once per motion, and in the two films with the most on-screen UI motion every audible attack sat on the music's grid, so the music "sounded" the motion. **Audible UI clicks were not confirmed in any of the ten mixes.** The closest case is Screen Studio, where two possible clicks at +6.4 and +9.3 dB land on the frame a "Private" toggle appears. These counts are lower bounds, since quiet clicks under music cannot be confirmed from a mix.

Tutorials agree on restraint. Film Editing Pro's first-pass mistakes were the same keyboard sound for every keystroke and the same pen sound for every stroke, and its rule is that "if you try to make every moment special none of them will be special" ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wcxw3BPSt3A)). Echoic Audio notes that "many sounds are heard only once" ([iZotope](https://www.izotope.com/community/blog/echoic-audio-part-3)). Density heuristics cluster low: one effect every 8 to 15 s for explainers ([Videotto](https://videotto.com/blog/add-sound-effects-and-meme-edits)), a creator's measured median of 6.5 per minute across six explainers with the shortest gap always longer than the sound ([note.com](https://note.com/samune_labo/n/n3727df03a197?hl=en)), and the warning that at 20 to 30 per minute viewers stop hearing them as signals (same source). Vendor guidance on demos says to keep clicks "for actions that matter" because "every click competes with the voiceover" ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/demo-sound-effects)), while also listing "silent product interactions that feel cheap" as a failure ([IRPR Sound](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/applications/product-videos/)). Read together, these say the story click should exist and be heard, and the decorative click should usually not exist.

The contrast with the mgpro film Torq is stark. Its cue sheet holds **191 cues in 30 s across 29 sound types**, each tied to a picture event by one timing file, with fixed per-type gains varied by only ±1 dB ([cues.py](</home/wopler/dev/torq-video/audio/cues.py>)). The professional films use three families, keep them under the music, and place them no more often near motion peaks than at random times. The earlier AI films also hit cuts 18.6 times above baseline against 1.18 for 2,666 professional cuts, and shared 42% of their strong transients between films against 4.6% for unrelated professional films ([film measurements](</home/wopler/dev/mgpro-research/research_notes/Sound design in motion graphics/real_films_measured.md>)). Counting cues alone does not separate human from machine; families, level against the music, coupling to every event and repetition across films do.

## Each sound type has its own source, length, level and budget

Levels below are given in two forms. "Over the bed" is the effect's peak short-term level against the median level of the music in the 1.5 s before it, the measure used on the ten films. Peak meter figures (dBFS) apply when voiceover anchors the mix, where the most quoted bands are voice -6 to -15 dBFS, music -18 to -22 dBFS alone and -25 to -35 under speech, and effects -10 to -20 dBFS with spikes to -8 ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/how-to-set-audio-levels-for-video/); [Epidemic Sound](https://www.epidemicsound.com/blog/audio-mixing-for-video/)). Frequency budgets are derived from the measured films and density heuristics above; no source publishes per-type counts for product ads.

### Story clicks sit 6 to 9 dB over the bed and land on the press frame

The best free click sources are Freesound CC0 recordings of real hardware and Kenney's designed clicks. Remotion's own `mouseClick` is Freesound 678248 by Pixeliota, CC0, about 0.397 s at 44.1 kHz ([Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/sfx/mouse-click)). The most downloaded CC0 mouse recordings include 347544 (Masgame: single, double and middle clicks "for animations or games"), 213004 (abstraktgeneriert, "suited to mouse clicks in an operating system"), 448086 (Breviceps), 683099 (florianreichelt) and 108336 (Qat, fast UI click), out of 543 CC0 results ([Freesound search](https://freesound.org/search/?q=mouse+click&f=license%3A%22Creative+Commons+0%22&s=Downloads+%28most+first%29)). For Apple hardware the closest free sounds are MacBook trackpad recordings: 118206 (MegaPenguin13), 467490 (neilraouf), 197169 (damjancd) and 332010 (hannagreen), from a 12 result search of which 10 are CC0, so each page's licence must be checked ([Freesound search](https://freesound.org/search/?q=trackpad+click&s=Downloads+%28most+first%29)). No free Magic Mouse recording exists; only paid packs list one. Mixkit's click category adds "Mouse click close", "Clear mouse clicks", "Select click" and "Interface device click" ([Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/click/)), and Pixabay has several one-second "Mouse Click" files from Universfield and SoundReality ([Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/search/mouse%20click/)).

A good click is short, dry and bright. Taps run 30 to 150 ms and interface cues under 300 ms ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-design-ui-sounds/)), with energy around 2 to 8 kHz and presence at 1 to 3 kHz so it reads on small speakers ([Cursa](https://cursa.app/en/page/sound-design-that-reads-on-small-speakers)). In the local CC0 library a good click falls 20 dB in 4 to 18 ms, attacks in under 1 to 3 ms and centres at 1.9 to 4.6 kHz with under 10% of its energy below 500 Hz ([mgpro library analysis](</home/wopler/.claude/skills/mgpro/sfx/ANALYSIS.md>)). Phone speakers roll off below about 150 to 200 Hz, so **high-pass every click at 150 to 200 Hz and keep it only if it still reads** ([Cursa](https://cursa.app/en/page/sound-design-that-reads-on-small-speakers)). A file that vanishes after the filter was mostly thump.

Level is where the owner's note and the sources meet. No standard number exists for clicks over music. One Remotion-oriented guide puts primary button clicks about **+6 dB over the music bed** ([skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh/jeremielim/skills/ui-sound-design)), Screen Studio's possible toggle clicks measured +6.4 and +9.3 dB over the bed, and Cursa advises nudging key effects up "on the exact frame of the action" and dipping music 1 to 3 dB rather than raising the effect overall ([Cursa](https://cursa.app/en/page/sound-design-that-reads-on-small-speakers)). A story click should therefore peak **6 to 9 dB over the bed**, with a 2 to 4 dB music dip of about 150 ms around it if it still hides; secondary clicks sit 4 to 8 dB below it. With voiceover, place the click between stressed words rather than raising it over them. Keep clicks centred, since phones play effectively in mono.

Timing is asymmetric. ITU-R BT.1359 puts detection at about 45 ms of sound lead and 125 ms of lag ([ITU](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1359-1-199811-I!!PDF-E.pdf)), and Netflix QC logs effects 2 or more frames off as an issue ([Netflix](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000533952-Sync-Effects)). So **the click transient goes on the frame where the button visibly depresses, or up to one frame after, never two frames before**, and the sync point is the transient, not the file start ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/how-to-sync-sound-effects-to-video)). Trim leading silence so the transient sits at sample zero. If the file holds a release, it can land 1 to 3 frames later, 6 to 10 dB quieter, on the frame the button springs back; that offset is frame arithmetic, since no source measured real mouse press-to-release gaps. Use 3 to 5 click takes, rotate them, and shift each by half to one semitone and a dB or two.

### Typing is cut from real bursts, never one key looped

Typing has the clearest repetition failure on record: the same keystroke on every key ([Film Editing Pro](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wcxw3BPSt3A)). The fix in that tutorial is to cut a 10 s typing recording into single keystrokes and use each once. Free material includes OpenGameArt's "Keyboard Soundpack #1" of typing and single keystrokes, CC0 ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/keyboard-soundpack-1-typing-and-single-keystrokes)), a second OpenGameArt pack with 32 single key presses and 10 typing takes at different velocities (licence not confirmed) ([OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/node/122745)), Freesound 729421 (MacBook Air M2 keys and trackpad) and 391541 (a trackpad click then typing), and Mixkit's 19 s "Typing on a laptop keyboard" ([Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/keyboard/)). The cheap paid option is shapeforms "Type", 226 sounds from four keyboards from $5.99 ([itch.io](https://shapeforms.itch.io/type)). Pixabay's style note matters for product films: "soft keyboard tones suit modern looks better than mechanical clacks" ([Pixabay blog](https://pixabay.com/blog/posts/free-and-high-quality-sound-effects-for-video-edit-453/)).

The working recipe, synthesised from the sources: cut 6 to 10 single keystrokes from one keyboard, never play the same file twice in a row, vary pitch by about 1 to 2 semitones and gain by 2 to 3 dB, and use a heavier stroke for space and enter. On-screen text often types one character per frame, faster than any human, so it should not get one sound per character. Place a stroke on every second or third character, or lay a short real burst under the word, so the rate stays under about 10 to 12 keys per second. In Contra's stems the typewriter thread ran a median 7.5 dB under the music ([Contra](https://contra.com/p/WPMQbb6O-sound-design-exploration-contra)), so typing belongs under the story clicks as texture, one burst per text field.

### UI taps and toggles stay short, dry and in one tonal family

Designed interface sounds (toggles, checks, confirmations, notifications) come from Kenney's UI Audio and Interface Sounds, Mixkit's "Modern click box check", "On or off light switch tap", "Positive interface beep" and "Cool interface click tone", and unfa's CC0 "Menu Sounds" (Freesound 244266) ([Kenney](https://kenney.nl/assets/ui-audio); [Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/interface/); [Freesound](https://freesound.org/search/?q=mouse+click&f=license%3A%22Creative+Commons+0%22&s=Downloads+%28most+first%29)). Keep each family (success, error) consistent in pitch and decay so the product "speaks with one voice", keep tails under a second, keep them mostly dry and narrow, since "heavy reverb on quick UI sounds increases masking" ([Pixabay blog](https://pixabay.com/blog/posts/free-and-high-quality-sound-effects-for-video-edit-453/); [media.io](https://www.media.io/audio-effects/quick-sound-effects.html)). Tonal cues should be pitched in the score's key, a toggle rising for on and falling for off ([heyheythere](https://heyheythere.itch.io/soft-ui-sfx/devlog/1685319/ui-sounds-that-dont-clash-pitch-them-all-from-one-pentatonic-scale)). Contra's digital UI layer sat a median 13.8 dB under the music, rising above it only in gaps ([Contra](https://contra.com/p/WPMQbb6O-sound-design-exploration-contra)). One chime per new panel, as Morphic suggests ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/demo-sound-effects)), and silence for hovers and small fades. If the real product makes no sounds, soft designed ticks that agree with each other read better than literal desktop clicks, because IRPR lists sounds that do not match the product as a mistake ([IRPR](https://sounddesign.irpr.agency/applications/app-videos/)).

### Pops mark appearances in groups, not per item

When "every text overlay gets a pop, the sounds lose meaning" ([SFX Engine](https://sfxengine.com/blog/common-sound-design-mistakes-in-video-editing)). The well-used CC0 pops are "Pop sound" by deraj (202230, 62,400 downloads), "blip-plock-pop" by onikage22 (240566), "Plop!" by Breviceps (447910) and "Bubble Pop.wav" by OGMeek (59978); most other top results are cartoony ([Freesound search](https://freesound.org/search/?q=pop&f=license%3A%22Creative+Commons+0%22&s=Downloads+(most+first))). Mixkit's "Explainer video pops whoosh light pop" and "Plastic bubble click" suit explainer appearances ([Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/whoosh/)). A good product pop is soft and short: in the local library pops rise 3 to 7 semitones, and the measured synthesis target is 70 to 110 ms at 740 to 990 Hz ([mgpro library analysis](</home/wopler/.claude/skills/mgpro/sfx/ANALYSIS.md>)). Place the transient a frame or two before the element reaches full scale, on the overshoot, which follows the "slightly before creates anticipation" advice ([Ocular Sounds](https://ocularsounds.com/blogs/sound-design-tips-tricks/whoosh-audio-effect-adding-motion-and-energy-to-your-edits)) but has no sourced offset. When several items appear, give the group one pop, or a short sequence that climbs 2 to 3 semitones per item; the game-audio "few percent" of pitch jitter (half to one semitone) is too small to read as a deliberate step ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-implement-footstep-variation-to-avoid-repetition)). Pops are supporting sounds, peaking well under the story clicks.

### Slides and swishes match the travel time and rotate through five or more takes

This is the sound the owner heard repeating, and the evidence names the cause twice: "a generic sweep pasted over every cut" ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide)) and the same whoosh on every transition ([Filmora](https://filmora.wondershare.com/audio-editing-tips/whoosh-sound-effect.html)). For cards, text and pictures sliding, the right material is short real air passes, not the "cinematic" whooshes that dominate Pixabay and Mixkit. The CC0 Freesound swooshes fit: Shinplaster 264777, Halgrimm 169867, florianreichelt 683101 ("quick woosh"), qubodup 59988 ("SWOSH-01"), dave.des 127197 ("fast simple chop 5") and PrdiChlp 181889, a soft reversed breath swell for gentle reveals ([Freesound search](https://freesound.org/search/?q=whoosh&f=license%3A%22Creative+Commons+0%22&s=Downloads+(most+first))). Sound Digitator's stick, badge and cat toy swings and SoundResponse's 20 free whooshes add recorded variety ([itch.io](https://sound-digitator.itch.io/whoosh-a-free-sound-library); [SoundResponse](https://www.soundresponse.net/?p=1815)). From Mixkit, "Air woosh", "Fast whoosh transition", "Arrow whoosh" and "Swirling whoosh" are UI scale; from Pixabay, "Whoosh Motion" (405445), "Whoosh Effect" (405447), "Whoosh Short Realistic" (379079) and "Simple Whoosh" (382724) ([Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/whoosh/); [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/search/whoosh/)). Skip "Swoosh Explosion", "Epic whoosh" and anything trailer-tagged for UI motion.

**Length must follow the move.** "A tiny card crossing 10% of the screen in 180 ms" needs a different envelope from a heavy 1.2 s pass, and a title reveal of 0.48 s leaves its last 80 ms empty for a separate landing ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide)); "a whoosh that outlasts its transition is the most common reason one feels wrong" ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/whoosh-sound-effect)). Real swishes are low "whums" with a whistle: 60 to 160 ms, centred at 190 to 600 Hz, while only designed airy transitions sit at 5 to 13 kHz and read as digital ([mgpro library analysis](</home/wopler/.claude/skills/mgpro/sfx/ANALYSIS.md>)). So a 180 ms card slide gets a 150 to 300 ms swish trimmed from a longer file, with its peak on the fastest frame or the settle frame. Build short, medium and long variants rather than time-stretching one file, which smears transients into metallic artefacts ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide)).

Level these as supporting sounds: Contra's transition layer ran a median 2.6 dB under the music, and Zella's rule for voice films is "roughly -6 dB relative to speech", adding that "if a viewer notices the whoosh consciously, it is too loud" ([Zella](https://zellahq.com/blog/sound-design-at-cuts-whoosh-impact-riser/)). Sound only the hero move of each scene; when several elements move together give the group one sound, and if muting a swish changes nothing, delete it ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide)). HeroUI's one whoosh every 3 s is the ceiling for a heavily designed film. Vary the path as well as the file: across, past, toward, away ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide)), and pan with the motion only for clear horizontal travel.

### Transitions mark section changes only, and skip cuts the music already marks

Scene transitions take longer whooshes, reverse swells and swipes. 99Sounds' "Cinematic Transitions" (50 WAV transitions by Goncalo Penas) and "99 Sound Effects", Mixkit's whoosh, transition and 23 swell effects, and the Sonniss bundles are the clean sources ([99Sounds](https://99sounds.org/?p=2387); [Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/swell/)); Zapsplat added 150 whooshes from Music for Video, credit required on the free tier ([Zapsplat](https://www.zapsplat.com/royalty-free-music-loops-and-whooshes-from-music-for-video)). For a transition of about half a second the whoosh runs 400 to 500 ms, starts just before the change and peaks on it ([Zella](https://zellahq.com/blog/sound-design-at-cuts-whoosh-impact-riser/)), with 2 to 6 frames of lead quoted by vendors, and a tail-less swipe suits hard cuts because a long tail blurs them ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/transition-sound-effects)). Pre-lapping the next scene's sound before the picture changes is the standard alternative ([PitchDrift](https://pitchdrift-productions.com/sound-design-tips-for-motion-graphics/)). The deciding rule is redundancy: **if the music already swells or resolves at the cut, a whoosh on top is redundant** ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/blog/guides/transition-effect-sound-video-edits)), and in a video that cuts every 4 to 5 s, sounding every join makes it "feel like a percussion track" ([Zella](https://zellahq.com/blog/sound-design-at-cuts-whoosh-impact-riser/)). Featured transitions measured 7 to 13 dB over the bed in the middle of the ten films.

### Paper and foley replace whooshes when the object is physical

For a card sliding, a page turning or a box flap folding, a short paper or cardboard sound is more specific than any whoosh. Freesound's CC0 "cardboard box" search returns 306 results, among them GrayJoy's "Cardboard Box Slides and Rustling" (344432), LeonsFlashlight's "Box open" (135014), newagesoup's taps (364932) and drop (339365), kd_jack's "Scratch in cardboard or wood" (271318) and florianreichelt's "Drawing with a pen" (407405) for line-draw animation ([Freesound search](https://freesound.org/search/?q=cardboard+box&f=license%3A%22Creative+Commons+0%22&s=Downloads+(most+first))). The Designer's Choice UCS Pack #34 FOLEY (cloth, hands, wooden box, pencil sharpener) is royalty free with credit optional ([Freesound pack 43548](https://freesound.org/people/designerschoice/packs/43548)), Nox_Sound releases foley in CC0 ([Freesound](https://freesound.org/people/Nox_Sound/)), Fiftysounds offers free paper sounds without attribution ([Fiftysounds](https://fiftysounds.com/royalty-free-music/paper.html)) and Sonniss 2026 names foley among its contents. Kenney's Impact Sounds supply soft wood and card landings. Search with "paper slide", "page turn", "paper rustle", "cardboard slide", "wood tap" and "card on table". Foley belongs to the mechanical family, which ran about 7 dB under the music in Contra, and Joe Vanz's crash course adds the human touches: pan with the screen movement and time-stretch with warp markers to match the ease curve ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GovWYxWNS54)). Physical events should be recordings: in a 2026 study, 18 audio professionals rated library samples above procedural sounds by 13.8 to 34.8%, with synthesis least believable for everyday actions ([arXiv 2605.31082](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.31082)).

### Impacts and risers come once or twice per film, trimmed and light on sub

Impacts come from Kenney's Impact Sounds, Mixkit's "Metal hit woosh" and "Movie whoosh impact presentation", 99Sounds' impacts, transients and punches, BOOM's processed impacts and Sonniss; EminYILDIRIM's cinematic hits on Freesound are mostly CC BY with 57 CC0 files ([Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/whoosh/); [Freesound](https://freesound.org/search/?f=username:%22EminYILDIRIM%22)). Risers come from Mixkit's 23 swells, Fiftysounds risers, BudgetPixel's "Rising Cymbal Swell", Pixabay's 12 s "Riser Wildfire" and PrdiChlp's CC0 breath swell ([Fiftysounds](https://fiftysounds.com/royalty-free-music/risers.html); [BudgetPixel](https://budgetpixel.com/sfx/rising-cymbal-swell-a57de9b9); [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/film-special-effects-riser-wildfire-285209/)). Product films need short versions of both. Trim risers to 1 to 3 s (one or two bars), start them about a second before the claim and **stop them dead when the moment lands** ([Zella](https://zellahq.com/blog/sound-design-at-cuts-whoosh-impact-riser/)); the measured risers peaked 6.6 to 9.2 dB over the bed. Impacts land on the contact frame, since "even 50 ms early reads as sloppy" (same source), and a boom "swells in slowly" to fill a held shot while an impact "lands on a specific frame" to mark a cut ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/boom-sound-effect)). Add a landing layer "only when the object visibly stops or appears" ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide)). Sub-heavy hits vanish on phones, so layer a short transient with content between 1 and 5 kHz on top ([Johnny Copland](https://johnnycopland.com/how-to-mix-bass-for-smartphone-speakers/)). Avoid the Vine boom, the braam and anything named "epic trailer"; three even, low impacts read "as controlled rather than epic" ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/cinematic-sound-effects)).

### The logo usually lands on the music's own hit

For a scored product film the cleanest logo moment is the track's own final hit or resolve on the logo frame, with at most a soft swish or air swell leading in, because if the music already resolves an extra effect is redundant ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/blog/guides/transition-effect-sound-video-edits)). Many productions layer "a musical hit over a designed impact for extra weight" ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/ai-glossary/stinger)). A melodic premade sting carries its own key and tempo and can clash; a non-tonal soft impact or shimmer is safer to layer. Free stings exist, such as BudgetPixel's AI-generated "Soft Marimba Logo Sting" (2.9 s, no attribution) and Mixkit's "Movie whoosh impact presentation" ([BudgetPixel](https://budgetpixel.com/sfx/soft-marimba-logo-sting-9642d4eb)), while ElevenLabs' free logo sounds require credit ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/sound-effects/logo)). Measured logo hits were the loudest moments: +9.2 dB over the bed at Screen Studio and +12.8 dB (16.9 dB over the film median) at Raycast after a 150 ms silence. The accent goes on the landing frame or one frame early, and the mark then holds 1.5 to 3 s with the tail ringing under it ([Ad Council](https://www.adcouncil.org/media-standards-guide/television); [Tas Gov](https://www.communications.tas.gov.au/styleguide/p_-_television/30_seconds_and_above)). Because of the trademark clauses above, a stock sting must never become a brand's reusable sonic logo.

## Human mixes vary every hit and make contrast by subtraction

### Tiers, dips and filters keep story sounds audible without harshness

The level order is settled even where numbers differ: voice, then foreground effects, then music, then ambience. Effects run -10 to -20 dBFS with occasional spikes to -8 and nothing above -6 ([PremiumBeat](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/how-to-set-audio-levels-for-video/)); Krotos puts footsteps and environmental sounds near -20 dB and hits up to -10 dB against music near -20 dB ([Krotos](https://krotos.studio/blog/how-to-balance-music-and-sound-effects)); Film Editing Pro's meter guide gives effects "anywhere from -10 dB to -30 dB" and the score -20 to -30 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Wcxw3BPSt3A)). One explainer creator keeps effects about 19 to 20 dB below the narration in loudness and sets them to one fixed clip volume rather than by ear each time ([note.com](https://note.com/samune_labo/n/n3727df03a197?hl=en)). That suggests three tiers: story effects (the key click, the reveal hit, the logo) around -10 to -12 dBFS, supporting effects (swishes, pops) around -14 to -18, and texture (typing threads, rustles, room tone) around -22 to -35.

Story effects cut through by **dipping the music briefly, not by pushing the effect**. DaVinci Resolve's ducker defaults to a 2.7 dB duck with 15 ms lookahead, 10 ms rise, 150 ms hold and 750 ms recovery, and its manual says 2 to 5 dB works "best most of the time" ([DaVinci Resolve Club](https://davinciresolveclub.com/davinci-resolve-audio-ducking/)). Joe Vanz sidechains the music to the effects track for the same purpose ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GovWYxWNS54)). The measured films show the bigger version: accents above 15 dB only came after the music was pulled down, so the contrast was made by subtraction. An earlier finding adds a warning: a dip with no musical cause reads as a fault, so a silence before a hit should be short (100 to 300 ms) and keep a tail or bed under it ([Phillips et al.](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/9193057)).

EQ follows the speakers. High-pass most effects around 80 to 100 Hz, keep no more than 3 to 5 key layers playing at once ([SFX Engine](https://sfxengine.com/blog/common-sound-design-mistakes-in-video-editing)), cut 200 to 400 Hz on whooshes that muddy voice and music, though "you don't have to do this with every single whoosh" ([Joe Vanz](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GovWYxWNS54)), and be conservative boosting 2 to 5 kHz because phones hype that band and harshness "becomes painful fast" ([Sonarworks](https://www.sonarworks.com/blog/learn/make-your-mix-sound-great-on-small-speakers)). Reverb glues rather than decorates: Vanz uses about 15% wet ("you don't want to hear the reverb, you just want to notice when it's not there"), while quick UI sounds stay dry. Check the mix on phones, laptops, in mono and at low volume, and run OverseerOS's "remove 50%" test: mute half the decorative effects and compare ([OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-sound-design)). Master to about -14 LUFS integrated with a -1 dBTP ceiling for web and social ([SFX Engine](https://sfxengine.com/blog/common-sound-design-mistakes-in-video-editing)); the Contra and HeroUI mixes measured -14.4 and -12.8 LUFS. Platforms turn loud audio down but do not reliably raise quiet audio ([APU Software](https://apu.software/tiktok-instagram-reels-loudness/)), and hot effect peaks drive the limiter and cost the music and voice their level.

### Variation, fades and small edits are what separate human from stock

The game-audio fix for the "machine-gun effect" transfers directly: **4 to 8 variations per sound type, random selection with no immediate repeats, and a few percent of random pitch and level per play** ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-implement-footstep-variation-to-avoid-repetition); [DAFx 2021](https://acris.aalto.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/67780224/DAFx20in21_paper_22.pdf)). OverseerOS extends it to video: vary sample, pitch, timing, stereo position, level and duration ([OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-sound-design)). Whole-file pitch shifting helps less than changing the timbre, so different recordings beat one recording at several pitches, and beyond 4 to 5 semitones the algorithm itself becomes audible ([Swell AI](https://www.swellai.com/blog/how-to-pitch-shift-in-audacity)). Krotos adds a cheap generator of new takes: combine the start of one whoosh with the tail of another, keeping both layers peaking together ([Krotos](https://krotos.studio/guide/4-whoosh-sound-effects-tips)). An untouched stock whoosh is itself listed as an amateur giveaway ([SFX Engine](https://sfxengine.com/blog/common-sound-design-mistakes-in-video-editing)), so every file gets at least a trim, a fade and a level set for its moment.

The small edits are consistent across sources. Trim the head, because files "may contain silence before the actual effect starts" ([Krotos](https://krotos.studio/blog/audio-trimming)); fade nearly every sound in and out, since abrupt starts and stops are a strong giveaway ([SFX Engine](https://sfxengine.com/blog/sound-design-tips-for-video-editors)); use "a short ramped fade-up rather than a hard volume boost" for builds ([Krotos](https://krotos.studio/blog/what-is-sound-design-in-video-editing)); reverse a sound so it leads into a cut; pitch a few semitones down for mass and up for small elements; keep narration and clicks centred and pan only what visibly travels ([Pro Sound Effects](https://blog.prosoundeffects.com/how-to-make-sound-effects-sit-better-in-a-mix)). OverseerOS's list of twelve mistakes is a usable final checklist: sound on every cut, effects over important words, the same whoosh repeated, music and effects fighting the voice, everything cinematic, no ambience, ambience too loud, no dynamic range, mixing in solo, mixing only on headphones, mastering to fix the mix, and adding effects because the video feels boring ([OverseerOS](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-sound-design)).

### A per-film download pipeline needs a pool, a ledger and a no-repeat rule

The evidence translates into a short set of rules for code that downloads premade sounds per film. **Pick three or four families per film before placing anything** (for example UI ticks, paper foley and soft air swishes), matched to the brand, and draw only from them. **Fetch a pool, not a sound**: 3 to 5 takes per story-click type, 6 to 10 keystrokes, 5 to 8 swishes from at least two sources, then rotate with no immediate repeats and small pitch and level jitter, keeping tonal sounds on scale notes instead. **Tag every cue with a tier** (story, supporting, texture) and mix by tier against the measured music level, so story clicks land 6 to 9 dB over the bed with a short duck and supporting sounds stay under it. **Trim each file to its move**: transient at sample zero, body equal to the travel time, fade on both ends. **Change the pool between films**, keeping a ledger of every file used and refusing to reuse a previous film's hero sounds, since repetition across films was the measured AI tell.

Access decides which libraries a script can touch. Kenney zips download with plain curl, though the hash in each URL changes when a pack updates, so the asset page should be scraped for the current link ([open library notes](</home/wopler/dev/mgpro-research/research_notes/Sound design in motion graphics/open_sfx_libraries.md>)). Sonniss bundles come once by official torrent or mirror and are curated locally into a private folder. Freesound needs an API key on every request, and originals (rather than lossy previews) need an authenticated download ([Freesound API](https://freesound.org/docs/api/authentication.html)). Pixabay forbids "bulk, large-scale or systematic copying" and automated collection for machine learning ([Pixabay Terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)), and Mixkit, 99Sounds, BOOM and Zapsplat are browser flows, so their files should be picked by a person and stored, not scraped. The local CC0 library of 1,126 sounds is thinnest exactly where the downloads matter most, with only 19 pops, 30 whooshes and 26 risers ([mgpro library analysis](</home/wopler/.claude/skills/mgpro/sfx/ANALYSIS.md>)). Every file needs a manifest line with source URL, author, licence and version, and download date, because Sonniss binds each file to the licence version current when it was downloaded ([Sonniss licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)). Only CC0 files may live in a repository other people can read.

## Conclusion

The two problems the owner heard are one mistake with two faces. A click too quiet to hear and a whoosh that repeats on every slide both come from treating sound as a one-to-one mapping from picture events to samples at a fixed gain. Human editors invert that mapping: they decide which few moments carry the story and make those unmistakable, then let the music and a quiet, varied texture carry everything else. The measured films make this concrete. Audible clicks were nearly absent from published SaaS films not because clicks are wrong, but because each one that survives is there for a reason and is loud enough to be read. A pipeline that ranks events by story weight before it chooses sounds will produce both fewer and clearer effects than one that sounds every event and then turns the result down.

The licence picture also shifts the work. The free sources that are safe for client ads and also scriptable are few (Kenney, Sonniss, Freesound CC0 through the API), and the best curated material at Mixkit, Pixabay and 99Sounds must be chosen by hand. That argues for a curated, private pool per sound family, built once with a licence ledger and refreshed between films, rather than a search at render time. The open questions are empirical: no source measures per-type effect levels in product ads, the click level of 6 to 9 dB over the bed rests on one guide and two uncertain measurements, and the next films should be measured the same way to check that the story clicks read on a phone speaker and that no hero sound repeats across films.
