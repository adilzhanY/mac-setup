# Premade motion sounds for motion graphics and product videos (whooshes, swishes, pops, transitions, foley, impacts, risers, logo stings)

Research date: 2026-10-31. Format note: each finding ends with its source link in parentheses. Licences are quoted as the source states them. Where a source could not confirm something, it is listed under Gaps rather than guessed.

## 1. Which sources have the best free whooshes, swishes and pops for subtle UI and card motion (not trailer whooshes)?

### Takeaway
For subtle card, text and UI motion the most useful free material is short (under about 1 s) air, cloth, stick and breath swishes, not the "cinematic" tagged whooshes that dominate Pixabay and Mixkit. The best verified free sources are the Freesound CC0 swooshes (qubodup, Shinplaster, Halgrimm, florianreichelt), Sound Digitator's organic "Mini Whooshes" (sticks, cat toy, badge), the 99Sounds "Cinematic Transitions" and "99 Sound Effects" packs, SoundResponse's 20 free whooshes, and Kenney's CC0 UI packs for clicks and pops. Pixabay and Mixkit are fine for quick picks but are skewed toward cinematic and trailer style.

## 2. How to choose, time, level and vary a motion sound (length, weight, material, pitch, pan, timing, level, variation)

### Takeaway
Match the sound to the motion's speed, mass, distance, direction and stop point; put the loudest point on the fastest moment or the cut; keep effects above the music only as far as needed; and never reuse an identical envelope. Build short, medium and long variants rather than heavily time-stretching one file.

### Cited Findings
- A whoosh should follow the motion's "speed, mass, direction, distance, and stopping point," not a generic sweep. Short bright envelopes read as fast; longer builds read as broad or deliberate; low-mid body reads heavy; thin air reads light; close motion has more detail and air, distant motion has softer highs and narrower image. ([Sonilo whoosh guide](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- Worked examples: "A tiny card crossing 10% of the screen in 180 ms" needs a different envelope from a heavy object passing over 1.2 s; a product fly-by: 1.15 s, fastest point at 620 ms, short dry release, no impact; a title reveal: 0.48 s, fast rounded attack, last 80 ms left empty for a separate impact. ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- Choosing type: large object crossing frame = full whoosh; fast card, swipe or light gesture = swoosh; scene change with no visible mover = transition sound; title or product landing = whoosh into impact; small element appearing = pop; and "if the movement already reads clearly, compare against no whoosh at all." ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- Timing: mark prepare (rises as motion begins), cross (loudest or brightest point, aligned with fastest movement or the cut centre) and release (tail). Nudge the cross one frame earlier and later and check at normal speed. Keep the tail clear of the next line, beat or ambience change. ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- "Time the peak of the whoosh to land on the cut"; too slow drags behind the picture, too fast reads as a click; match weight to subject. ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-make-whoosh-sounds/))
- A whoosh slightly before a visual change creates anticipation; one exactly on the cut reinforces clarity. ([Ocular Sounds](https://ocularsounds.com/blogs/sound-design-tips-tricks/whoosh-audio-effect-adding-motion-and-energy-to-your-edits))
- "A whoosh that outlasts its transition is the most common reason one feels wrong." ([Morphic whoosh guide](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/whoosh-sound-effect))
- Scale layers to the object: bigger means lower and slower. ([Krotos](https://www.krotosaudio.com/?p=1006981))
- Pitch: rising pitch can suggest acceleration or approach, falling pitch moving away or losing energy (not fixed rules). Filter movement suggests distance change. Use time-varying pan only when the picture supports it, and compare against a centred or narrower version. ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- Avoid heavy time-stretching (smears transients, metallic artefacts); build separate short, medium and long variants instead. ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- Layer by function: air for broad motion, texture for material and distance, optional tonal layer for identity, and a landing layer (impact, click or pop) "only when the object visibly stops or appears." Reserve the widest, longest cue for the largest movement so smaller gestures still feel intentional. ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- When layering, make layers the same length and have them "peak at the same time"; combining the start of one sound with the tail of another produces new variants. ([Krotos 4 whoosh tips](https://krotos.studio/guide/4-whoosh-sound-effects-tips))
- Variation: avoid reusing an identical envelope across repeated edits; vary path (across, past, toward but stopping short, away), speed and distance. ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- Mix checks: level-match variants before judging (louder seems better); check low-mid body on phone speakers, harsh highs, mono collapse, and that the sequence still works with the cue muted. ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- Level against music: one rule of thumb puts music around -20 dB with effects from -20 dB (ambience) to -10 dB (hits), peaks higher, context dependent. ([Krotos balance guide](https://krotos.studio/blog/how-to-balance-music-and-sound-effects)) Effects sit under dialogue and above music; dip music a few dB for an impact rather than muting it. ([Morphic mixing guide](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/how-to-mix-sound-effects-with-music)) One editor's practice: music at -12 dB, about 75% of effects peaking around -6 dB, explosions near 0 dB. ([Creative Cow](https://creativecow.net/?p=1599474))
- Sync tolerance: Netflix QC treats effects 1 frame or less early or late against on-screen action as minor. ([Netflix Backlot via search](https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000533952-Sync-Effects))

### Inferences
- Practical length rule from the Sonilo examples: trim the whoosh so its audible body roughly equals the on-screen travel time (a 180 ms card slide gets a 150 to 300 ms swish, not a 2 s file), with the peak on the fastest frame or the frame the object settles, and a short tail.
- For a pop on a scale-in, place the transient a frame or two before the element reaches full scale (on the overshoot, not after settle); this is consistent with the "slightly before creates anticipation" advice and the 1 frame tolerance, but no source gives an exact offset.
- Variation recipe that combines the sourced advice: rotate 3 to 5 different files per sound type, pitch each by a small amount, trim start and tail differently, and only layer a landing element where the object visibly stops.
- Pan should follow direction only for clear horizontal travel; for UI motion near centre, a narrow pan is safer for phone and mono playback.

### Gaps
- No source gave a specific frame offset for pops versus scale animation, or an exact semitone range for pitch variation; both remain practitioner judgement.
- No source gave a LUFS target specifically for SFX relative to music in short product films.

## 3. The overused whoosh and the "every element gets a whoosh" problem: sparse use rules

### Takeaway
Editors and sound guides agree: match each sound to a visible movement, skip it where music already marks the moment, use transitions and SFX sparingly, and remove any sound whose absence does not make the piece worse. No source gives a numeric "whooshes per minute" figure.

### Cited Findings
- Warns against "a generic sweep pasted over every cut". ([Sonilo whoosh guide](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- If the background music already swells or resolves right at the cut, a whoosh on top is redundant; match sound length roughly to the transition and adjust frame by frame. ([Sonilo transition guide](https://sonilo.com/blog/guides/transition-effect-sound-video-edits))
- Beginners stack effects while professionals remove sounds that don't contribute; remove a sound if the piece feels the same or better without it. ([Motion design sound blog](https://portfollio-9ip.pages.dev/blog-sound-design.html))
- Build sound around motion, acceleration and contact rather than putting the same effect on every transition; a single mismatched impact makes surrounding motion feel less consistent. ([Pippit](https://www.pippit.ai/blog/sound-design-for-motion-graphics))
- A Trinity College Dublin dissertation applies "less is more" principles (simplicity, negative space, hierarchy) to animation sound design. ([TCD dissertation](https://publications.scss.tcd.ie/theses/diss/2021/TCD-SCSS-DISSERTATION-2021-015.pdf))
- Editor Jonny von Wallstrom: stop overusing similar flashy transitions; they date a project and distract from the story. ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2017/05/watch-5-mistakes-you-might-avoid-editing))
- Use transitions, graphics and sound effects sparingly so they do not become grating. ([vidIQ summary](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/editing-mistakes-youtubers-make-that-viewers-hate))
- Well placed whooshes are not consciously noticed by the audience. ([Ocular Sounds](https://ocularsounds.com/blogs/sound-design-tips-tricks/whoosh-audio-effect-adding-motion-and-energy-to-your-edits))
- A Reddit r/sounddesign post asks whether a short edit built mostly on layered whooshes, impacts and transitions feels "cohesive or overdone", showing the concern is common. ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1qkvuxc/snapshots/2026-01-23T23%3A20%3A40.964255Z))
- Counter view: any effect can work when it accomplishes a specific goal; the issue is use, not the effect. ([DVinfo forum](https://dvinfo.net/forum/open-dv-discussion/30597-transitions.html))

### Inferences
- A defensible rule for a 20 to 60 s product film: sound the hero moves (the biggest movement of each scene, the main cut points, the logo), let small secondary motions ride on the music, and when many elements animate together give the group one sound, not one each. A rough budget of one designed motion sound every few seconds, with at most one impact and one riser per film, follows from the sources' "reserve the biggest cue for the biggest move" and "sparingly" advice; the numbers are inference, not sourced.
- Mute test from Sonilo ("check the sequence with the cue muted") doubles as the sparse rule: if muting a whoosh changes nothing, delete it.

### Gaps
- No source quantified how many whooshes per 30 s is normal in product ads.
- No School of Motion article on the topic surfaced in searches.

## 4. Logo stings and resolves: premade sources, and when editors use a musical hit from the track instead

### Takeaway
Free logo stings exist (Mixkit, BudgetPixel, Uppbeat, itch.io packs) but quality and licences vary. Industry descriptions say productions often layer a musical hit over a designed impact; if the music already resolves on the logo, an added sting is redundant. No authoritative editor interview comparing the two approaches was found.

### Cited Findings
- A stinger can be musical (brief scored accent or chord) or sound-effect based (designed impact or boom); "many productions combine the two, layering a musical hit over a designed impact for extra weight." ([Morphic stinger glossary](https://morphic.com/ai-glossary/stinger))
- A short end-card sign-off differs from a longer reveal, where a longer swell and impact build under an animated reveal. ([Morphic logo sounds](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/logo-sound-effects))
- If music already swells or resolves at the cut, an extra whoosh is redundant (applies equally to the logo moment). ([Sonilo transition guide](https://sonilo.com/blog/guides/transition-effect-sound-video-edits))
- Title or product landing pattern: whoosh into an impact, with the final 80 ms of a 0.48 s whoosh left empty for the separate impact. ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- BudgetPixel lists free AI-generated logo stings (example: "Soft Marimba Logo Sting", 2.9 s; "Digital Glass Logo Sting"), "royalty-free, no attribution required", free for commercial use. ([BudgetPixel marimba](https://budgetpixel.com/sfx/soft-marimba-logo-sting-9642d4eb); [BudgetPixel glass](https://budgetpixel.com/sfx/digital-glass-logo-sting-e359669f))
- ElevenLabs logo sounds require attribution to elevenlabs.io on the free plan. ([ElevenLabs logo](https://elevenlabs.io/sound-effects/logo))
- Dark Fantasy Studio "STINGERS Sfx pack" on itch.io described by the seller as 100% royalty free. ([itch.io](https://darkfantasystudio.itch.io/stinger-sfx-pack))
- Uppbeat has logo stings with download buttons; licence not shown in results. ([Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/sfx/cinematic-logo-heavy-metal-guitar-riff/8167/24461))
- Mixkit has "Movie whoosh impact presentation" (0:03) and "Explainer video pops whoosh light pop" (0:01) usable as small reveal elements. ([Mixkit whoosh](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/whoosh/))
- Stock libraries (Soundstripe) tag logo reveal packs as "musical" and as "sound design"; one pack is "complex and textured hits with some swishes". ([Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/library/sound-effects/62391))

### Inferences
- For product films with a scored track, the cleanest logo moment is usually the track's own final hit or resolve chord placed on the logo frame, with at most a soft swish or air swell leading in. A separate premade sting fits when the music cannot be edited to land there, or when the brand wants a reusable sonic logo.
- Premade stings carry their own key and tempo, so they can clash with the track; a non-tonal designed hit (soft impact, shimmer) is safer to layer over music than a melodic sting.

### Gaps
- No professional editor or sound designer interview directly comparing "music hit vs SFX sting" for logos was found; the sourced material is mostly vendor glossaries.
- Licences for Uppbeat and the itch.io stinger pack were not verified.

- Paid references: A Sound Effect "Paper & Cardboard" 500 sounds, $49 (page links a freebie pack); Storm Wave Audio "Paper Sound Effects Vol. I" (rips, crushes, rustles). ([A Sound Effect](https://www.asoundeffect.com/?p=52771); [itch.io](https://stormwave-audio.itch.io/paper-sound-effects-vol-i))

### Inferences
- For paper or card motion (a card sliding, a page turning, a box flap folding) a short paper slide or cardboard rustle often replaces a whoosh entirely and sounds less generic; GrayJoy 344432 and LeonsFlashlight 135014 are good starting files.

## 6. Risers and impacts: free sources and keeping them tasteful in product (not trailer) contexts

### Takeaway
Free risers and swells come from Mixkit (23 swells), Fiftysounds, Output's old "Free Rises", Freesound reverse cymbals, Pixabay, and BudgetPixel. Tasteful use means short, tied to a real visual event, landing on the frame, music dipped a little rather than muted, and the biggest cue reserved for the biggest moment.

### Cited Findings
- Mixkit has 23 free swell effects including reverse style, under the Mixkit License. ([Mixkit swell](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/swell/))
- Fiftysounds risers: free for personal and commercial use, no attribution. ([Fiftysounds risers](https://fiftysounds.com/royalty-free-music/risers.html))
- Output "Free Rises" (2015): free WAV riser and tension pack; availability today not confirmed. ([Bedroom Producers Blog 2014](https://bedroomproducersblog.com/2014/12/10/free-riser-sound-effects/))
- Freesound reverse cymbal crash sweep (ID 134941, Alastair_Pursloe) and a riser (ID 334521); licence must be checked per page. ([Freesound 134941](https://freesound.org/s/134941); [Freesound 334521](https://freesound.org/s/334521))
- Dark Fantasy Studio "RISERS Sfx pack": 50 cinematic risers, tension builders, atmospheric swells (price and licence not shown). ([itch.io](https://darkfantasystudio.itch.io/risers-sfx-pack))
- BudgetPixel free swells, for example "Rising Cymbal Swell" and "Airy Choral Swell Riser", no attribution. ([BudgetPixel](https://budgetpixel.com/sfx/rising-cymbal-swell-a57de9b9))
- Pixabay "Riser Wildfire" by SoundReality, 0:12. ([Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/film-special-effects-riser-wildfire-285209/))
- Freesound "Whosh, Reversed/Reverbed breath" by PrdiChlp (ID 181889, CC0) is a soft reverse swell. ([Freesound CC0 whoosh search](https://freesound.org/search/?q=whoosh&f=license%3A%22Creative+Commons+0%22&s=Downloads+(most+first)))
- Impacts sources: Mixkit "Metal hit woosh" (0:01) and "Movie whoosh impact presentation" (0:03); trailer level "Epic movie trailer whoosh impact" (0:09). ([Mixkit whoosh](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/whoosh/))
- Use a landing layer (impact, click or pop) only when the object visibly stops or appears; product fly-by example uses "no impact". ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- Hits around -10 dB with music near -20 dB as a rule of thumb; music dips a few dB under an impact rather than muting. ([Krotos](https://krotos.studio/blog/how-to-balance-music-and-sound-effects); [Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/how-to-mix-sound-effects-with-music))
- Check low-mid body is not muddy on phone speakers and highs are not piercing. ([Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/ai-music/whoosh-sound-effect-guide))
- A single mismatched impact makes surrounding motion feel less consistent. ([Pippit](https://www.pippit.ai/blog/sound-design-for-motion-graphics))

### Inferences
- In product films, trim risers hard (1 to 3 s rather than 8 to 12 s files), cut the sub layer, and end them exactly on the reveal frame or the downbeat; a riser that runs into music that already builds is redundant (the same logic as Sonilo's "music already swells" rule).
- Prefer soft, short impacts (a muted thud, a click, a wooden knock) over trailer booms; one impact per film, on the product or logo landing, is consistent with "reserve the biggest cue for the biggest move".

### Gaps
- No professional source specifically about keeping risers and impacts tasteful in product ads (as opposed to trailers) was found; the guidance above combines general mixing sources.
- Licences of Freesound IDs 134941 and 334521 and the Dark Fantasy Studio packs were not verified.
