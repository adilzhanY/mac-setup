# Open, CC0 and permissively licensed sound effect libraries for commercial client videos

Research date: 2026-10-27. Scope: free SFX sources usable in paid product ads (motion graphics: whooshes, UI clicks, impacts, foley, textures), with licence terms quoted from primary pages where they could be fetched.

## Q1. Catalog: licence terms, contents, format and download route per source

### Takeaway
Only three sources combine a clearly commercial, attribution free licence with direct curl downloads and no account: Kenney (CC0, small OGG packs), the Sonniss GDC bundles (royalty free custom licence, many GB of pro WAV) and the Sonic Pi sample set (CC0). Pixabay, Mixkit, 99Sounds and BOOM free packs are commercially usable without credit but are web downloads with "no standalone redistribution" clauses. Zapsplat, Uppbeat, Universal UI Soundpack and Freesound CC BY need credit or a paid tier. BBC RemArc stays non commercial.

**Freesound (per sound licences)**
- Uploaders choose CC0, CC BY 4.0 or CC BY NC 4.0. Sampling+ is legacy and "is being removed by creative commons"; Sampling+ sounds stay until owners relicense. [Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)
- CC0: "pretty much what you want", including selling, but not claiming authorship. CC BY: commercial use allowed with credit; "you should always mention the original creators of the sounds". CC BY NC: "you can't earn any money with the piece of work you create!" Sampling+: commercial advertising not allowed. [Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)
- Downloading requires a logged in account. A Generative AI preferences panel was added in July 2026; preferences do not change the licence. [Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)
- API: an API key must be added "to every request you make to the API" (search uses token=YOUR_API_KEY); OAuth2 is for non read only actions. [Freesound API authentication](https://freesound.org/docs/api/authentication.html)
- A forum reply on Freesound states monetized YouTube counts as commercial use, so NC sounds cannot be used there without permission. [Freesound legal forum (search snippet)](https://freesound.org/forum/legal-help-and-attribution-questions/39864)

**CC0 legal code (for the CC0 sources above)**
- Waiver covers use "for any purpose whatsoever, including without limitation commercial, advertising or promotional purposes" (section 2); "No trademark or patent rights held by Affirmer are waived" (4a); "Affirmer offers the Work as-is and makes no representations or warranties of any kind" (4b); "Affirmer disclaims responsibility for clearing rights of other persons" (4c). [CC0 1.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en)

### Inferences
- CC0 4(b) and 4(c) mean CC0 is only as clean as the uploader's honesty. Kenney (self made, single author) and Sonic Pi (curated) are low risk; random Freesound or OpenGameArt CC0 uploads carry a small risk of re uploaded copyrighted material, so keep the source URL and licence for each file used in a client job.
- Kenney packs are OGG, short and game oriented. Fine for UI clicks, toggles, ticks, glitch and light impacts; too thin for cinematic whooshes, risers or textures.
- Sonniss bundles are the only free source with professional, high resolution cinematic material (whooshes, impacts, ambiences, foley) at scale and with a no credit commercial licence.

## Q2. Can raw files live in a private repo, and can a film using them be sold?

### Takeaway
Selling a finished film or ad is allowed by every commercial licence here (Kenney, Sonniss, Pixabay, Mixkit, 99Sounds, BOOM, Zapsplat with credit or Gold, Soundly). Keeping raw files in a private repo is unconditionally safe only for CC0 (Kenney, Sonic Pi, CC0 Freesound). Licences that forbid standalone distribution allow it for your own use but sharing that repo with other people is a grey zone, and Zapsplat explicitly forbids sharing files with anyone.

### Cited Findings
- CC0 waives all copyright "for any purpose whatsoever", so redistribution of raw files (public or private) is allowed. [CC0 1.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en)
- Sonniss: finished projects "may be shared, published and sold in the normal way", but sounds may not be distributed "as sound effects", and edited versions "remain licensed material". [Sonniss GDC bundle licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)
- Pixabay: no "Standalone" distribution, where Standalone means no creative effort applied. [Pixabay Terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)
- 99Sounds: may not "redistribute (even for free) the audio files", "alone or as a separate attachment to your work". [99Sounds licence](https://99sounds.org/license/)
- Zapsplat: "grants a single user", "must not be shared with any person", "must not be distributed in any form". [Zapsplat Standard License PDF](https://s3.amazonaws.com/zapsplat-assets/zapsplat-standard-license.pdf)
- Soundly: no resale of raw or edited sounds. [Soundly FAQ](https://getsoundly.com/faq/how-can-I-use-the-sounds/)

### Inferences
- A private repo used only by the licensee (one person, or the licensing company) is not "distribution" in the ordinary sense, so Sonniss, Pixabay, Mixkit and 99Sounds files can be kept there. If the repo is shared with contractors, other studios or a client, that is arguably supplying the sounds as sounds; the safe pattern is to commit only CC0 raws and keep non CC0 raws in an untracked local cache with a manifest (source URL, licence, date) in git.
- A public repo must hold only CC0 (or CC BY with credit kept) raw files. Sonniss, Pixabay, Mixkit, 99Sounds, BOOM and Zapsplat raw files must never be pushed publicly.
- Handing a client project files (a Remotion project with an audio folder) to the client is a redistribution risk under Zapsplat and 99Sounds; hand over the rendered video plus a cue sheet instead.

### Gaps
- No licence here defines whether a company wide licence covers several employees (Sonniss names "the Licensee", which may be a company; not confirmed).
- No court or vendor statement found on private repos specifically.

## Q4. Ranking for motion graphics (whooshes, UI clicks, impacts, foley, textures)

### Takeaway
1) Sonniss GDC bundles, 2) Kenney Interface/Impact/UI/Digital, 3) Pixabay SFX, 4) Mixkit SFX, 5) BOOM free packs (Cinematic Series, Processed Impacts), 6) 99Sounds, 7) Freesound CC0 subset, 8) Remotion SFX and Sonic Pi, then credit or paid sources (Zapsplat with credit or Gold, Universal UI Soundpack CC BY, Uppbeat Business). Exclude BBC RemArc, YouTube Audio Library and Freesound CC BY NC for client ads.

### Cited Findings
- Sonniss: pro vendor material, tens of GB, commercial, no credit. [Sonniss GameAudioGDC](https://sonniss.com/gameaudiogdc/)
- Kenney Interface Sounds: click, tick, toggle, switch, select, glitch, scroll, open/close, confirmation, error (100 files). Impact Sounds: glass, metal, wood, plate, punch, soft impacts in three weights (130 files). Verified from zip contents. [Kenney Interface Sounds](https://kenney.nl/assets/interface-sounds); [Kenney Impact Sounds](https://kenney.nl/assets/impact-sounds)

- Zapsplat explicitly lists "Commercials" as allowed, with credit unless Gold. [Zapsplat Standard License PDF](https://s3.amazonaws.com/zapsplat-assets/zapsplat-standard-license.pdf)

| 5 | BOOM free packs | BOOM free terms | No | Cinematic and processed impacts; pro quality | No (cart/newsletter) |
| 6 | 99Sounds | 99Sounds licence | No | Textures, designed sounds | No |
| 7 | Freesound CC0 only | CC0 | No | Everything, uneven | API key / login |
| 8 | Remotion SFX, Sonic Pi | MIT pkg + per sound (often CC0), CC0 | No | A few whooshes, beats, textures | Yes |
| 9 | Zapsplat | Zapsplat Standard | Yes unless Gold | Huge catalogue, MP3 on free tier | No |
| 10 | Universal UI Soundpack | CC BY 4.0 | Yes | UI | No (itch) |
| Exclude | BBC RemArc, YouTube Audio Library, Freesound CC BY NC, Sampling+ | Non commercial or YouTube only | | | |

### Inferences
- Ranking weighs licence cleanliness first, then pro quality, then scriptability. A practical motion graphics kit is Sonniss for whooshes, impacts and textures plus Kenney Interface Sounds for UI ticks, with Pixabay or Mixkit as manual top ups.
- Kenney OGG files should be converted to 48 kHz WAV for editing; they are lossy at about 96 to 240 kbps, fine for short UI blips but not for exposed, loud hero hits.

### Gaps
- Quality judgements for Pixabay, Mixkit, 99Sounds and BOOM are based on their catalogues and reputation, not on listening tests.

## Q5. Historical licence changes to watch

### Takeaway
Three changes matter: Sonniss moved to licence v2.0 on 27 August 2026 with an AI training ban; Freesound is retiring Sampling+ and added AI preferences in July 2026; Pixabay terms were updated 20 October 2026 and prohibit bulk copying.

### Cited Findings
- Sonniss v2.0 effective 27 August 2026, applies to downloads on or after that date, adds "No AI Training or Usage". [Sonniss GDC bundle licence](https://sonniss.com/gdc-bundle-license/)
- Freesound: CC BY 4.0 and CC BY NC 4.0 now; Sampling+ being removed; AI preferences panel July 2026. [Freesound FAQ](https://freesound.org/help/faq/)
- Pixabay "Last updated: 20 October, 2026". [Pixabay Terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/)
- BBC archive expanded from about 16,000 to 33,000+ files but kept the non commercial RemArc terms. [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/music/the-bbc-sound-effects-archive-over-33000-free-samples); [CDM](https://cdm.link/bbc-gives-away-16k-wav-sound-effects-disallows-using/)
- Zapsplat's licence PDF on S3 dates from 30 June 2021. [Zapsplat Standard License PDF](https://s3.amazonaws.com/zapsplat-assets/zapsplat-standard-license.pdf)

### Inferences
- Record the download date and licence version with each file: Sonniss bundles downloaded before 27 August 2026 fall under the earlier licence, later downloads under v2.0.
- Pixabay's licence history (it moved from CC0 to its own Pixabay License in 2019) means old "Pixabay is CC0" claims in tutorials are outdated; this is from background knowledge and was not verified in this session.

### Gaps
- The text of the Sonniss licence before v2.0 was not retrieved, so the exact differences are unknown beyond the AI clause.
