# Sound for product and brand motion films: studios, sound designers and case studies

Scope note: research run on 2026-10-27, about 30 search and fetch calls. Citation format below is "claim: [Source](URL)". Primary sources (studio case pages, award entries, the designers' own words) are marked where available. Many target companies (Stripe, Linear, Notion, Vercel, Raycast, Arc, Framer, Airbnb, Duolingo) returned no public sound credits or making-of material at all, see the Gaps sections.

## 1. What studios and sound designers say about their approach

### Takeaway
The studios that do have public statements agree on three things: bring sound in early (at concept, not after picture lock), treat music and sound design as one discipline that serves the idea, and design sound as a system with a job to do rather than as decoration. Most of these statements come from Antfood and Wes Slover (Sono Sanctus / Sanctus Audio). Primary statements from Echoic, Unminus, Box of Toys Audio, Smart Sound, Mutato, Simon Bennett and the in-house SaaS teams could not be found.

### Cited Findings

Antfood (New York, Sao Paulo, Amsterdam; frequent partner of BUCK)
- Antfood wants to be involved "from concept and pre-production", so sound can shape the work before decisions are locked (Motionographer interview, primary): [Motionographer, "The other half with Antfood" (2018)](https://motionographer.com/2018/09/05/the-other-half-with-antfood/)
- They treat sound design, music and audio post as one discipline, asking "what sound can do for the idea or message", and argue audio can "inspire direction, editorial and creative decisions across the board" rather than rescue a finished project: [Motionographer (2018)](https://motionographer.com/2018/09/05/the-other-half-with-antfood/)
- On cost and status: "music is often the least appreciated aspect of production, both in terms of budget and consideration of time": [Motionographer (2018)](https://motionographer.com/2018/09/05/the-other-half-with-antfood/)
- Process is wide exploration then narrowing: "We pitch a wide variety of ideas in order to open up a conversation about what this thing could become." Some projects see "30, 50 or 100 ideas" and they "settle on the 99th or go back to the very first one": [Motionographer (2018)](https://motionographer.com/2018/09/05/the-other-half-with-antfood/)
- Tools: projects often start in Ableton (sometimes Max/MSP or Max for Live) and are finished in Pro Tools; the studio uses a 1974 Neve 80 series console, analog synths and a mic collection mainly to capture performances and spark ideas: [Motionographer (2018)](https://motionographer.com/2018/09/05/the-other-half-with-antfood/)
- Antfood now markets sonic brand work under "strategy, identity and governance" (BASYS brand systems): [Antfood site](https://antfood.com/); [sonic.antfood.com](https://sonic.antfood.com/)

Wes (Wesley) Slover, Sono Sanctus / Sanctus Audio
- Sono Sanctus tagline: "Don't let audio be an afterthought": [Motion Hatch podcast ep. 013 show notes](https://motionhatch.transistor.fm/episodes/013-how-to-think-about-sound-design-music-as-an-animator-w-wesley-slover)
- He starts from what the audio must do (capture attention, reduce friction, build confidence, or stay in the background) before asking how it should sound: [By The Booth, "Sound: The Invisible Brand Layer"](https://www.bythebooth.com/insights/creative-tangent-wesley-slover-sound-the-invisible-brand-layer)
- He avoids leading with temp tracks because "demo love can be really hard to break": [By The Booth](https://www.bythebooth.com/insights/creative-tangent-wesley-slover-sound-the-invisible-brand-layer)
- Stock vs custom rule of thumb is story arc: stock music usually holds one mood, custom suits a real journey or precise musical "hit" moments; for simple energy a strong library track can be the better choice: [By The Booth](https://www.bythebooth.com/insights/creative-tangent-wesley-slover-sound-the-invisible-brand-layer)
- Sonic branding "shouldn't stop at an audio logo"; the logo defines the feeling and palette, which then extends into UX sounds so marketing and product feel like one brand. His example is Carbon (Sanctus Audio), from audio logo to UX sounds. His work is heard in Discord, Asana and Under Armour products: [By The Booth](https://www.bythebooth.com/insights/creative-tangent-wesley-slover-sound-the-invisible-brand-layer)
- Warning on trends: brands chasing a trendy sound to project confidence can end up sounding unserious; goal is audio that stays "relevant, subtle, and supportive": [By The Booth](https://www.bythebooth.com/insights/creative-tangent-wesley-slover-sound-the-invisible-brand-layer)
- On talking about sound with clients: "it's just so hard to talk about sound. We don't always have good language for it." He now prototypes audio behaviour so teams can "try it and react": [By The Booth](https://www.bythebooth.com/insights/creative-tangent-wesley-slover-sound-the-invisible-brand-layer)
- SoundOn project: Sono Sanctus invited the motion community to submit GIFs for them to score; about 60 submissions, over a year of work. Slover: the motion community "is constantly pumping out tons of awesome work that needs noise": [Motionographer, "SoundOn with Sono Sanctus" (2019)](https://motionographer.com/2019/04/11/soundon-with-sono-sanctus/)

Ambrose Yu (composer and sound designer, frequent Ordinary Folk collaborator)
- Describes himself as making "sounds for things that move or tell stories"; Motion Awards 2017 sound category judge and winner in Music Composition for Animation ("Imaginary Friends Society: Feeling Sad"): [Motion Awards judge page](https://2017.motionawards.com/judge/ambrose-yu/)

Apple in-house sound design (UI sound, not launch films)
- Apple's sound designers Billy Sorrentino, Hugo Verweij and Kelly Jacklin discussed Apple's sound philosophy (Tritone, Apple Watch sounds, notification tones) on the Twenty Thousand Hertz podcast; the A Sound Effect write-up contains no direct quotes: [A Sound Effect, "Behind the sound of Apple"](https://www.asoundeffect.com/behind-the-sound-of-apple/)
- Apple WWDC session on designing sound (UI and haptics): [WWDC17 "Designing Sound"](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2017/803/)

### Inferences
- The shared principle across Antfood and Slover is "sound is decided at concept, by job". For a code-made product film this maps to: write the sound brief (what each moment must make the viewer feel or notice) alongside the storyboard, not after render.
- Slover's stock vs custom rule is directly useful: if a film has an arc and hit points (reveal, logo), the music must be cut or composed to those points; a single-mood bed is only fine for short, flat energy pieces.
- Antfood's "30 to 100 ideas" process shows that sonic quality at the top end comes from many divergent sketches, then picking. A synth pipeline can imitate this cheaply by generating several palettes and comparing.

### Gaps
- No primary interviews found for Echoic, Unminus, Box of Toys Audio, Smart Sound, Mutato, Simon Bennett, Giant Ant, Tendril, Animade, Jam3, Antibody, ManvsMachine, Gunner or Oddfellows about sound. Echoic has a Behance profile and an iZotope interview ([Echoic on Behance](https://www.behance.net/echoicaudio/info); [iZotope, Echoic Audio part 1](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/echoic-audio-part-1)) which I did not read in full.
- No public sound credits or philosophy found for in-house teams at Stripe, Linear, Notion, Vercel, Raycast, Arc, Framer. Stripe's Brand Studio exists and runs Sessions, but no audio credits surfaced ([Stripe job posting](https://jobs.generalcatalyst.com/companies/stripe/jobs/39588970-events-creative-producer-brand-studio)).
- The Motion Hatch episode with Slover covers budgeting, team building and music licensing per its notes, not sync technique; the audio itself was not transcribed.

## 2. Case studies: specific films and what their sound does

### Takeaway
Of the requested companies, only Apple, Google/YouTube and Netflix have documented sound stories. The documented films cluster into three sound strategies: (a) tightly scored music plus SFX locked to fast animation (Apple iMac Pro, Google Design), (b) a soundtrack built from the brand's own sounds (Apple "Start Up", Apple Music "Made by Sound"), and (c) music-first pieces where the score drives the animation (Ordinary Folk). Public sources almost never state BPM or SFX counts; those details are a gap.

### Cited Findings

1. Apple, "Power of Creativity on the new iMac Pro" (BUCK direction, Antfood music and sound)
- Antfood wrote "tightly scored music and sound design to complement the fast paced, often erratic, animation", built around a "heavy synthy beat that develops" to signal the machine's power. Won 2019 ADC Awards Bronze, Craft in Motion/Film: Sound Design. No BPM or SFX list stated: [Antfood case page](https://antfood.com/work/apple-imac)

2. Apple event opener, "Start Up: 45 Years of Apple Sounds" (TBWA\Media Arts Lab LA), D&AD 2022, Sound Design and Use of Music category
- Idea: use a Mac to build a song using only sounds from Apple devices. The team mined Apple's 45 year sound catalogue, physically re-recorded some sounds for depth, then a producer built a track where every component is an Apple sound: "No fillers. No mixing studio." It opened the event with energy: [D&AD entry](https://dandad.org/awards/professional/2022/236414/event-opener-start-up-45-years-of-apple-sounds)
- Signature sounds used as the instrument palette (the brand's UI sounds become the music). Award level not shown on the page.

3. Apple Music, "Made by Sound" rebrand film (October 2026)
- The new logo was "physically shaped by an original composition" by Fred again.. and NEST Acoustics and "captured entirely in camera"; Apple: "Discover how sound brought the Apple Music logo to life": [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/21/apple-music-unveils-new-design-rebrand-with-logo-made-by-sound/)
- A secondary report says it is a 78 second film and the rig moved liquid at the frequency of the notes: [iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/102951/apple-music-unveils-new-brand-identity-made-by-sound-video) (not verified against Apple's own post).

4. Apple, "Behind the Mac: Skywalker Sound" (documentary short about Foley artists, Cannes Lions entry)
- [Cannes Lions / Love the Work entry](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/behind-the-mac-skywalker-sound-1540543); [StarWars.com](https://starwars.com/news/behind-the-mac-skywalker-sound). Sound detail of the film itself not documented in these sources.

5. YouTube sonic logo "Buckle Up" (Antfood sound, BUCK animated logo)
- About 3 seconds. Elements: a "deep dive" bass as a threshold into YouTube; an upward glissando for joy; a two-note melodic signature matching the two syllables of "YouTube", with a champagne-pop accent on "You"; a subtle tactile door-unlocking element nodding to DIY creator culture; a warm synth pad on a major 7th chord: [Antfood Labs, "Crafting YouTube's First Sonic Brand"](https://www.antfood.com/labs/crafting-youtubes-first-sonic-brand)
- Sync to picture: BUCK's animation goes CRT power-up, then expanding playhead and timeline bar, then left to right motion forming the icon, then the logotype slides into lockup; the sound's "story-driven" pillar asked for a short sound with a clear beginning and end: [Antfood Labs](https://www.antfood.com/labs/crafting-youtubes-first-sonic-brand)

6. YouTube Shorts sonic variant
- Same structure as the core logo in a distinct form, part of one flexible system across products, ads and sub-brands: [Antfood Labs](https://www.antfood.com/labs/crafting-youtubes-first-sonic-brand)

7. Google Design, "Design and Material Explorations" (Zelig Sound), D&AD Yellow Pencil 2023, Sound Design and Use of Music
- Zelig Sound did "an intense phase of sonic exploration" driven by the motion, edit and design, asking "Is it music or sound design, or both, or neither?"; goal "to delight and surprise the viewer". Jury: the work is "blurring the lines between music and sound design" and adds personality and charm to the animation through detail: [D&AD entry](https://dandad.org/awards/professional/2023/236787/google-design-design-material-explorations); also a Cannes Lions entry: [Love the Work](https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/campaigns/google-design-design-material-explorations-1540374)

8. Google Cloud Next anthem (Antfood with BUCK)
- Original anthem fusing jazz, rock and electronic music, "an eclectic ode to creative and ingenuity"; Antfood did music, sound design and mix. Year not stated on the page despite the URL slug "2025": [Antfood case page](https://antfood.com/work/googlecloudnext2025/)

9. Google, "What is Google TV?" (BUCK direction and production, Antfood original music and sound design)
- Credit only, via an aggregator search result; no sound description found: [Antfood work index search hit, see Antfood site](https://antfood.com/)

10. Google, "Search On 2020 (OF Montage)" by Ordinary Folk
- Vimeo montage, sound not described in sources found: [Vimeo](https://vimeo.com/498126087)

11. Netflix "ta-dum" sonic logo (Lon Bender, 2015)
- Brief from Netflix VP Product Todd Yellin: tension and release, not too electronic, quirky, "screams Netflix" without saying it. Bender made 20 to 30 options (door sounds, strange instruments, production sounds, even a goat bleat). The knocks are his wedding ring on a cabinet (surface varies between reports); the bloom is reportedly a backwards guitar chord called "The Blossom". Accounts differ on whether a focus group or Yellin's daughter decided: [Twenty Thousand Hertz episode](https://www.20k.org/episodes/netflix); [Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2020/08/07/ta-dum-how-the-netflix-sound.html/amp); [/Film](https://slashfilm.com/netflixs-signature-launch-sound-was-almost-the-bleating-of-a-goat). Later extended for cinemas by Hans Zimmer: [Filmstarts (German)](https://www.filmstarts.de/nachrichten/18532087.html)

12. Mastercard sonic brand (2019)
- Built with McCann plus neurologists, psychologists, musicologists, composers and musicians over roughly 18 months to two years. Goals: "memorable", "hummable", "versatile", "simple, neutral, likeable". Architecture: a 30 second melody as DNA, a 3 second version for ads, a 1.6 second burst, and a short acceptance sound at checkout; local adaptations in many genres and tempos (Mike Shinoda involved): [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/09/07/sonic-branding-logo-audio-sound-mastercard); [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/news/mastercard-branding-now-includes-a-mogo/41809); [Mastercard, "Inside the science and success of sound"](https://www.mastercard.com/mea/en/news-and-trends/stories/2024/inside-the-science-and-success-of-sound.html); [Muse by Clio](https://musebyclios.com/music/hear-mastercards-new-brand-melody-in-various-apps-styles-and-places/)

13. Ordinary Folk, "Instrumental Visual Poem" (Ambrose Yu music, Avikali Lomavatu design, Jorge Canedo Estrada CD), 2024
- Music first: Yu composed the piece first, the team split it into a four-section "sound chart", and shapes were designed and animated to follow the music's rhythm. Canedo Estrada: "What if we allowed the melodies to choreograph a ballet of shapes?" No SFX or silence mentioned: [STASH](https://www.stashmedia.tv/exploring-the-dance-of-sound-and-imagery)

14. Ordinary Folk, "Nightdrive" (Ambrose Yu)
- Light, glass and water imagery set to Yu's rendition of Thom Yorke's "Dawn Chorus", a mood piece: [STASH](https://www.stashmedia.tv/ordinary-folk-invites-you-to-join-them-for-a-nightdrive-in-new-short-film/)

15. Ordinary Folk, "Mainframe" (Ambrose Yu music and sound design)
- Credit confirmed, sound not described: [STASH](https://www.stashmedia.tv/ordinary-folk-rescues-mainframe-motion-project-from-the-bin-of-destiny/)

16. "Welcome to Synth" explainer (The Furrow animation, Sanctus sound, with Twenty Thousand Hertz)
- Credit confirmed via STASH; detailed sound breakdown not read: [STASH](https://www.stashmedia.tv/welcome-to-synth-explainer-by-the-furrow-and-sanctus/); [Abduzeedo](https://www.abduzeedo.com/welcome-synth-illustration-motion-design-retro-vibe)

17. BUCK x Antfood, AMP Awards animation (2017)
- Self-parody of making music for advertising, built around a well timed animated fart joke (comic SFX timing as the punchline): [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/buck-antfood-amp-awards-animation-070617)

18. Headspace spot with BUCK Amsterdam
- Search snippet from LBB says the music is composed of four different moods, mirroring the app's core values; the page itself returned 403 so this is unverified: [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/work/64363); BUCK case page: [BUCK, Headspace Members](https://buck.co/work/headspace-members)
- Caution: "Headspace Studio" (Montreal and LA immersive audio, Jean-Pascal Beaudoin, Viktor Phoenix) is a different company from the Headspace meditation app: [MIT CMS event](https://cms.mit.edu/event/sound-first-rule-immersion-viktor-phoenix-jean-pascal-beaudoin/)

19. Honda, "The Other Side" (Factory sound design), Cannes Lions 2015 Gold and Silver for sound design
- Jury cited that the film making craft "had to be of the highest standard": [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/factory-wins-gold-silver-cannes-lions-for-sound-design)

20. Figma Config opening films (2024 and 2025)
- Animated by Copenhagen studio Relay with Figma's in-house brand studio (motion design lead Chad Colby; Config 2025 creative lead Leandro Castelao). No music or sound credit found: [Figma blog, Config 2025 identity](https://www.figma.com/blog/how-we-shaped-the-visual-identity-for-config-2025/); [Figma blog, Config 2024 branding](https://www.figma.com/ja-jp/blog/config-2024-branding/)

### Inferences
- Pattern for product launch films that win sound craft awards: the music is "tightly scored" to animation cuts, and one or more signature sounds come from the product or brand itself (Apple device sounds, YouTube's two-syllable motif). This is more distinctive than a generic library bed.
- Sonic logos in these cases are 1.5 to 3 seconds, have a clear start and end, often map syllables of the brand name to notes (YouTube), and include one tactile, physical-sounding detail (ring knock for Netflix, door unlock for YouTube) to feel human rather than synthetic.
- Music-first (compose, then animate to a section chart) is a real method used by Ordinary Folk for art pieces; launch films more often go picture-first then tight scoring.

### Gaps
- No sound descriptions found for: Stripe Sessions openers, Linear release videos, Notion launch videos, Vercel Ship, Raycast, Arc (The Browser Company), Framer, Airbnb, Duolingo brand films, Google Pixel launch films. These companies do not appear to publish audio credits, and searches returned nothing. A next step would be reading YouTube and Vimeo descriptions of each video directly.
- BPM, key and SFX counts are not stated in any source found, for any film. Do not invent them.
- Apple keynote product films (iPhone, Watch reveal films) have no public sound designer interviews in these searches.
- Material Design's sound guidelines page (m2.material.io/design/sound) returned no text when fetched, so Google's written sound principles are not captured here.

## 3. How studios build a sonic identity or sonic logo

### Takeaway
Documented processes share a sequence: audit the competitive sound landscape, translate brand pillars into sonic attributes, sketch many options, choose a short motif with a clear start and end, then expand it into a flexible system (lengths, genre variants, UX sounds) with governance.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube (Antfood): began with "a deep audit of the existing sonic branding landscape" to find open space; translated four brand pillars into sonic strategies (Human: textured, organically imperfect; Connected: a transitional gesture; Expressive: dynamic range in size, distance, tone, intimacy; Story-driven: short with a clear beginning and end); target was recognisability even when the viewer is not looking at the screen; BUCK animated the logo to the sound; then rolled out across devices and adapted for Shorts: [Antfood Labs](https://www.antfood.com/labs/crafting-youtubes-first-sonic-brand)
- Netflix (Lon Bender): a tight emotional brief (tension, release, quirky, not too electronic), 20 to 30 divergent options, a found physical sound as the core, then audience testing: [Twenty Thousand Hertz](https://www.20k.org/episodes/netflix); [Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2020/08/07/ta-dum-how-the-netflix-sound.html/amp)
- Mastercard: multidisciplinary team including neuroscience, five guiding principles (relatability and inclusiveness listed first), a long melody as DNA with 3 second and 1.6 second cuts, local genre adaptations: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/09/07/sonic-branding-logo-audio-sound-mastercard); [Mastercard](https://www.mastercard.com/mea/en/news-and-trends/stories/2024/inside-the-science-and-success-of-sound.html); [amp on Mastercard's "10-layer" plan](https://ampsoundbranding.com/amp-news/2022/10/17/inside-mastercards-10-layer-sonic-branding-plan)
- Slover: identity is a system, logo then UX sounds, so marketing and product feel like one brand: [By The Booth](https://www.bythebooth.com/insights/creative-tangent-wesley-slover-sound-the-invisible-brand-layer)
- Antfood also made CBS's sonic branding and an Instacart identity spanning marketing and product: [shots](https://shots.net/news/view/antfood-produces-new-sonic-branding-for-cbs); [Antfood, Instacart](https://www.antfood.com/work/instacart)

### Inferences
- For a product film made in code, a minimal "sonic identity" is: one 2 to 3 note motif tied to the product name or logo reveal, one tactile foley-like sound, and one pad/chord colour, reused in UI sounds if the product has any.

### Gaps
- Exact notes, key and tempo of the YouTube, Netflix and Mastercard logos are not stated in the primary sources read.

## 4. What separates award winning sound for motion from average work

### Takeaway
Juries and practitioners reward sound that is inseparable from the picture: detail that adds personality to the animation, blurred lines between music and SFX, a concept carried by the sound itself (brand sounds as instruments), and a clear idea rather than polish alone. No jury published an explicit rubric in the sources found.

### Cited Findings
- D&AD 2023 Yellow Pencil jury on Zelig Sound for Google Design: "blurring the lines between music and sound design", adding personality and charm to the animation through its detail: [D&AD](https://dandad.org/awards/professional/2023/236787/google-design-design-material-explorations)
- D&AD 2022 Apple "Start Up": concept-led, every component from Apple's own sounds, "No fillers. No mixing studio.": [D&AD](https://dandad.org/awards/professional/2022/236414/event-opener-start-up-45-years-of-apple-sounds)
- ADC 2019 Bronze, Craft in Motion/Film Sound Design for tightly scored music and SFX on fast, erratic animation (Apple iMac Pro): [Antfood](https://antfood.com/work/apple-imac)
- Cannes 2015: SoundCloud's audio recreation of the Berlin Wall won the Radio Grand Prix, a concept-driven audio piece: [radioinfo](https://radioinfo.com.au/hotoffthenet/soundclouds-ominous-audio-recreation-takes-radio-grand-prix-cannes/)
- Cannes 2015 Honda "The Other Side" sound design Gold and Silver (Factory), jury: craft "had to be of the highest standard": [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/factory-wins-gold-silver-cannes-lions-for-sound-design)
- Cannes Lions has a dedicated "Excellence in the Craft of Sound Design" in Film Craft; 2026 shortlist had 11 spots, including denis. (Denis Kilty) for AIB: [LBB Online](https://lbbonline.com/news/denis-Cannes-Lions-2026-Excellence-Sound-Design)
- Clio runs a Film Craft: Music and Sound Design category: [Clio](https://clios.com/?p=9516)
- Motion Awards run Sound categories (Music Composition for Animation, etc.), with Ambrose Yu as a 2017 judge: [Motion Awards](https://2017.motionawards.com/judge/ambrose-yu/)
- Practitioner view (Antfood): average work treats audio as post rescue; strong work involves sound from concept: [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2018/09/05/the-other-half-with-antfood/). Practitioner view (Slover): avoid temp-track "demo love", custom score for arcs with hit moments, subtle and supportive over trendy: [By The Booth](https://www.bythebooth.com/insights/creative-tangent-wesley-slover-sound-the-invisible-brand-layer)

### Inferences
- Working checklist derived from the above: (1) every visual accent has a deliberate sonic answer or a deliberate silence, (2) SFX are tuned to the music's key and rhythm so they read as part of the score, (3) at least one sound is specific to the brand or product, (4) the mix leaves space (restraint), (5) the logo hit has a clear beginning and end.

### Gaps
- No Cannes, D&AD or Clio jury document stating sound design criteria was found. Statements are per-entry jury comments only.
- No source in this run discussed silence explicitly as a judged quality; the "use of silence" question remains unanswered from primary sources.
- Under Armour "Rule Yourself" (Michael Phelps, Droga5, sound by Q-Department) was surfaced as a Film Craft Grand Prix in one search snippet labelled "2013-era", which conflicts with the 2016 campaign date; not verified.
