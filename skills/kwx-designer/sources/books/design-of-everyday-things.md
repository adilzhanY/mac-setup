# The Design of Everyday Things (Don Norman, revised edition 2013)

## What was read, and where

The book itself was not read (no legal free copy exists online; the PDF mirrors that search engines return are unauthorised and were avoided). Norman's ideas were read from his own writing and from close secondary sources:

- Norman, "Affordances and Design", jnd.org essay: https://jnd.org/affordances-and-design/
- Norman, "Signifiers, Not Affordances", Interactions, Nov/Dec 2008, author's version PDF hosted by TU Eindhoven: https://www.alice.id.tue.nl/references/norman-2008.pdf (read in full via pdftotext)
- Wikipedia summary of the book's principles and the seven stages of action: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Design_of_Everyday_Things
- NN/g articles that apply the book: the two gulfs, slips, mistakes, mental models.
- Secondary mention of Norman, "Natural User Interfaces Are Not Natural", Interactions, May/June 2010 (the ACM page refused access; key claims taken from a quoting blog post).

## Key lessons

1. **Two properties of good design:** discoverability (what actions are possible, what state is it in) and understanding (what does it mean, how is it used).
2. **Affordance is a relationship** (Gibson); what designers control on screens is the *perceived* affordance. "All screens afford touching: only some detect the touch and are capable of responding." The cursor is feedback, not an affordance.
3. **Signifiers over affordances.** "Forget affordances: What people need, and what design must provide, are signifiers." A signifier is any perceivable cue, deliberate or accidental, that can be interpreted meaningfully.
4. **Social signifiers:** traces of other people's behaviour (a crowded train platform, desire lines across a lawn) tell us how to act. On screens: popularity, recency, presence of others.
5. **Constraints:** physical, logical, cultural (and semantic). Cultural constraints are learned conventions: "Convention severely constrains creativity", yet breaking major conventions tends to fail.
6. **Four principles for screen design** (from "Affordances and Design"): follow conventions, use words, use metaphor carefully, keep one coherent conceptual model so what is learned in one place applies everywhere.
7. **Mapping:** layout of controls should mirror what they control (the stove).
8. **Feedback:** immediate and informative, not excessive.
9. **Seven stages of action:** goal, intention, specify, execute; perceive, interpret, compare. They define the gulf of execution (from goal to the right action) and the gulf of evaluation (from system state to knowing whether the goal was met).
10. **Errors are design failures.** Slips (right plan, wrong execution) and mistakes (wrong model) need different fixes, and neither is fixed by blaming or training the user.
11. **Gestural interfaces** need the same basics: feedback, explicit hints of possible actions, guides. Gestures are ephemeral and leave no record, so failures teach nothing.
