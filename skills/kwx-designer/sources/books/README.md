# Canonical reading list

The books and long sources that working designers credit, who recommends each one and why, whether it can be read legally for free, and which notes file in this folder holds the lessons. Research done 2026-10-27. No pirate sites were used (no libgen, z-library, Anna's Archive or PDF mirror sites); paid books were studied only through official samples, the authors' own free writing and talks, and reviews.

## How the list was built

Recommendations were gathered from:

- **Stripe Design Team** reading list (39 books): https://www.stripe.press/recommended/design-books.html
- **Anthony Hobday** (independent product designer): "Design books I recommend" and "Short design books I recommend": https://anthonyhobday.com/blog/20260408 ; "The best WWDC videos about interface design": https://anthonyhobday.com/blog/20220614
- **Hacker News** "learn UI/UX design" threads: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26932020 (101 points) and https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34149083 , plus the 172 HN comments that mention Refactoring UI (via hn.algolia.com)
- **Interviews and the designers' own sites:** Rauno Freiberg (Lovers Magazine interview; rauno.me), Emil Kowalski ("Developing taste"), Jesper Kouthoofd of Teenage Engineering (SFMOMA), Jony Ive (foreword to the Phaidon Rams monograph), Karri Saarinen of Linear (secondary summaries only).

What was **not** found: no published reading list from Apple's, Linear's, Vercel's, Airbnb's or Figma's design teams. Rauno Freiberg says he learned by reverse engineering code, not from books; Emil Kowalski's advice is to study great work and the people your heroes admire. For these studios, their own public writing (below, section C) is the closest thing to "the book that taught them".

## A. Core books, in reading order for interface craft

| # | Book | Who recommends it and why | Free? | Notes file |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Practical Typography**, Matthew Butterick | Standard HN and developer recommendation; Stripe lists his *Typography for Lawyers*. The fastest route to good body text, with numbers. | Yes, free online (pay what you want) | `practical-typography.md` |
| 2 | **Refactoring UI**, Adam Wathan and Steve Schoger | The most cited visual design book on HN for developers; Goodreads 4.64. Tactics with numbers for hierarchy, spacing, colour, depth. One HN warning: it teaches one aesthetic. | No (paid). Free: table of contents, preview chapter on colour, reviews, Schoger's tips | `refactoring-ui.md` |
| 3 | **The Design of Everyday Things**, Don Norman | Most recommended book in HN UX threads (4 + 2 recommenders); Stripe list. Affordances, signifiers, mapping, feedback, mental models. | No. Free: Norman's own essays (jnd.org, Interactions 2008 paper) | `design-of-everyday-things.md` |
| 4 | **Don't Make Me Think**, Steve Krug | Hobday's recommended list and HN threads (3 + 2 recommenders). Usability without jargon: scanning, mindless clicks. | No. Free: publisher sample pages | `dont-make-me-think.md` |
| 5 | **The Elements of Typographic Style**, Robert Bringhurst | Stripe list; "the typographers' bible" (Zapf); "the finest book ever written about typography" (Hoefler and Frere-Jones); HN classic. Print focused. | No. Free: Richard Rutter's *Elements of Typographic Style Applied to the Web* | `elements-of-typographic-style.md` |
| 6 | **The Vignelli Canon**, Massimo Vignelli | Stripe list; Hobday's short books list. Discipline, grids, two type sizes, white space, timelessness. | Yes, free PDF from the author (RIT Vignelli Center) | `vignelli-canon.md` |
| 7 | **The Shape of Design**, Frank Chimero | Hobday's short books list. Why over how, craft as care, delight as empathy plus surprise plus clarity. | Yes, free online | `shape-of-design.md` |
| 8 | **Dieter Rams: Ten Principles for Good Design** / **Less but Better** / **As Little Design as Possible** | Stripe list (de Jong's book); Hobday (Less but Better); Jony Ive wrote the Phaidon foreword. The root of Apple's and Braun's restraint. | Principles free (Vitsoe); books paid | `dieter-rams-ten-principles.md` |
| 9 | **Resilient Web Design**, Jeremy Keith | Web craft canon; matches Rauno's "work with the material". Honest materials, layers, progressive enhancement. | Yes, free online (CC BY-SA) | `resilient-web-design.md` |
| 10 | **The Humane Interface**, Jef Raskin | HN UX threads; the classic on modes, locus of attention, "intuitive equals familiar". | No. Free: course notes, Raskin's papers | `the-humane-interface.md` |
| 11 | **About Face**, Alan Cooper et al. | Listed in Hobday's interaction design catalogue; standard interaction design text. Posture, excise, intermediates. | No. Free: Wiley sample | `about-face.md` |

## If you only read five

1. Practical Typography (free): body text, the biggest single lever.
2. Refactoring UI: hierarchy, spacing, colour, depth tactics.
3. Vercel Web Interface Guidelines plus Rauno's guidelines (free): the details checklist.
4. Apple's Designing Fluid Interfaces and Animate with Springs (free): how motion should feel.
5. The Vignelli Canon (free): restraint and discipline as a value system.
