# Kurtenbach and Buxton (1993), The Limits of Expert Performance Using Hierarchic Marking Menus

- **Citation:** Gordon Kurtenbach and William Buxton, Proceedings of InterCHI '93, pp. 482-487.
- **Copy read:** text version on Bill Buxton's own site (billbuxton.com/MMExpert.html). A scanned PDF is also hosted by Autodesk Research.
- **Used in:** `editors/shortcuts-and-command-search.md`, `editors/case-studies.md`.

## What a marking menu is

A radial menu that can be used without being displayed. Novice: press and wait about 1/3 s, the menu pops up under the pointer, stroke toward an item (submenus open along the way), release to select. Expert: press and move immediately, drawing the same stroke (a "mark"); no menu appears. Because the physical movement is "identical", menu use rehearses the mark.

## Experiment

12 right-handed students, pen and mouse, menus of breadth 4, 8 and 12 and depth 1 to 4, labels chosen to be memorable (compass and clock positions). Points scheme rewarded speed and penalized errors.

## Results

- Response time grew roughly linearly with breadth and depth. Average 1.69 s with pen, 2.07 s with mouse.
- Error rate, not time, is the limit. Under 10 percent errors for 4-item menus up to depth 4 and 8-item menus up to depth 2. Beyond breadth 8 and depth 2, "selection becomes error-prone, even for the expert".
- Off-axis items (diagonals) were slower and less accurate than on-axis items. The pen handled off-axis better than the mouse.
- Breadth and depth trade roughly evenly within the acceptable range.
- Earlier work: marks about 3.5 times faster than menu selection.

## Design recommendations

Put frequent items on axes; keep menus within 4x4 or 8x2; choose breadth and depth by meaning; keep the pop-up menu as fallback; consider error cost; use marking menus where screen space is short or attention is on the content.
