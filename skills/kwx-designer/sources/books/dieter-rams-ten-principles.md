# Dieter Rams: ten principles for good design

- **Status:** the principles are published free by Vitsoe. The books (*Less but Better*, 1995; *Dieter Rams: As Little Design as Possible*, Phaidon, with a foreword by Jony Ive; *Dieter Rams: Ten Principles for Good Design*, Cees de Jong) are paid.
- **Legal source:** https://www.vitsoe.com/us/about/good-design
- **Read:** the ten principles with their explanations (Vitsoe), October 2026.
- **Who recommends it:** *Ten Principles for Good Design* is on the Stripe Design Team list; *Less But Better* is on Anthony Hobday's short books list. Jony Ive wrote the foreword to the Phaidon monograph and has credited a Braun product from his childhood as formative; Apple's industrial design language is widely traced to Rams' Braun work.

## The ten principles

1. **Innovative.** Innovation develops alongside technology, never as an end in itself.
2. **Makes a product useful.** It must satisfy functional, psychological and aesthetic needs; remove anything that distracts from usefulness.
3. **Aesthetic.** Things we use every day affect our wellbeing; only well executed objects can be beautiful.
4. **Makes a product understandable.** It clarifies the product's structure; at best it is self explanatory.
5. **Unobtrusive.** Products are tools, "neither decorative objects nor works of art"; design should be neutral and restrained to leave room for the user's self expression.
6. **Honest.** It does not make a product seem more innovative, powerful or valuable than it is, and does not manipulate.
7. **Long lasting.** It avoids being fashionable and so never looks antiquated.
8. **Thorough down to the last detail.** "Nothing must be arbitrary or left to chance." Care and accuracy show respect for the user.
9. **Environmentally friendly.** It conserves resources and minimizes physical and visual pollution.
10. **As little design as possible.** "Less, but better": concentrate on the essential, do not burden products with non-essentials. Back to purity, back to simplicity.

## How they translate to interface rules

| Principle | Interface rule |
|---|---|
| Useful (2) | Every element serves the screen's purpose; remove distractions |
| Understandable (4) | Self explanatory controls; signifiers over instructions |
| Unobtrusive (5) | Chrome is neutral and quiet; content and the user's work are the colour |
| Honest (6) | No dark patterns, no fake urgency, no glass or motion that implies capability the product lacks |
| Long lasting (7) | Avoid trend effects on core surfaces; trends only in the 10% novelty budget |
| Thorough (8) | Every state, every edge case, every value defensible |
| Little design (10) | Fewest typefaces, colours, effects and words that do the job |
