# Paul Stamatiou: Craft

- **Who:** product designer, nine years on Twitter's design team, later startups; long running personal site.
- **Status:** free essay.
- **Legal source:** https://paulstamatiou.com/craft ("Thoughts on elevating product quality", November 2022)
- **Read:** the full essay, October 2026.

## Key lessons

**Mindset**
- Put your name on the work; small teams "willingly put their name on it, taking accountability for what's great and what needs to be improved."
- "Don't rely on a future release to clean up today's mess." V1.1 cleanups rarely happen because priorities shift.
- Quality is a way of working from problem choice through sunsetting, not a final coat of paint or extra animation.
- Know when to hold the bar and when to compromise for real business needs.

**Defining quality**
- Broad: accessibility, performance, ease of use, reliability, durability, scalability, and thoughtful details that make people feel someone considered them.
- Set a team quality bar; a productivity tool weights reliability and speed, a consumer app weights delight and detail.
- Separate work types (visual refinement, performance bugs, crashers) and budget time for each every sprint so cosmetic work is not always crowded out.
- Compounding: one alignment fix does not matter, a dozen together are noticeable.
- Do not label quality work "polish" or "debt"; those labels send it to a backlog where it dies.

**Process**
- Replace MVP with SLC: Simple, Lovable, Complete. A release is a full slice of cake with every layer.
- "Just an experiment" is not an excuse; experiments ramp to 100% with their flaws.
- Pause launches that miss the bar; run a retrospective with an owner when quality slips.
- Leave time after research to refine, build and live with the design before shipping.
- Sunset low use features so the product stays fast and clear.

**Exploration**
- Prototype for fidelity; interactive prototypes reveal motion and feel that static mocks hide.
- Do not reach for a design system too early in a new product; it pushes toward familiar patterns before you know what the product is.
- Ask how another company would solve it, sketch that, see what it reveals.
- Figma for hunches; real signal comes from building and using it.

**Design and engineering**
- "Please, never treat design as something you just handoff to engineers." Design is an ongoing conversation.
- Make iteration fast: designers can run builds, get dogfood builds quickly, try many animation values.
- Route specialised work (complex collection view animation) to engineers with that skill.
- Designers who code test their own work and communicate precisely.

**QA and culture**
- Someone owns design QA (pixel and motion issues), and that work is rewarded because it is thankless.
- Tie quality to promotion criteria and celebrate great work.
- Leadership must back it, or individuals skip issues they expect nobody to notice.
