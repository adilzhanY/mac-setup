# Teenage Engineering: Jesper Kouthoofd interview

- **Status:** free interview.
- **Legal source:** SFMOMA, "Stay Curious, Stay Naïve: An Interview with Teenage Engineering's Jesper Kouthoofd": https://www.sfmoma.org/read/stay-curious-stay-naive-an-interview-with-teenage-engineering-jesper-kouthoofd/
- **Read:** full interview, October 2026. Search results also covered his October 2026 Highsnobiety interview saying TE will stop making synths ("the products aren't the business"); not used for rules.

## Lessons

- **Self imposed rules make freedom:** "I stick to rules and try to understand them, because then I can become free again." TE uses simple geometric shapes, a 40 colour RAL palette (fewer, faster choices than Pantone's endless blues) and German industrial standards, treating them as already solved problems.
- **Colour has fixed meaning across products:** orange or red always means record. Shapes pair with colours: triangle yellow, square blue, circle red.
- **Lowercase only in product text,** which he calls democratic: capitals give some words undue weight.
- **German plus Italian sensibility;** Otl Aicher (Ulm School) as an idol for how to think about design, not only for the work.
- **Question conventions** (he would replace the typographic point with millimetres).
- **Physical objects and tactile memory** of knobs and keys; people will keep wanting things they can handle.
- **Ethos:** "stay curious, stay naïve, and try new things." "It's about efficiency in the process and the expression."
- A TE designer elsewhere described the aesthetic as "no fuzz": as little design as possible, simple geometry, a basic palette.

## Rules taken for the skill

- Fix a small palette and give each colour one meaning across the whole product.
- Constrain shapes and type before designing; constraints speed decisions and create a recognizable identity.
- Typographic case is a design decision with a voice (all lowercase reads friendly and flat; Title Case reads formal).
